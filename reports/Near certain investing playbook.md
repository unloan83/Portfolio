# Switch to an index core at ₹0 tax

**The short answer:** put most of your money in **one Nifty 50 index fund, Direct plan, Growth option**, bought in rupee amounts from the mutual-fund section of your broker app or the fund house's website. Keep a small, capped group of stocks on the side. Hold a fixed share in a debt fund and in gold. Check the mix twice a year and send new money to whatever is below target. Use the yearly ₹1.25 lakh tax-free allowance on long-term gains every January to March. For your ₹6,75,074 portfolio (27 stocks, 7 ETFs, no debt, prices of 26 Sep 2026), the repo's lot-by-lot calculation shows the whole move costs **₹0 in capital-gains tax**, plus about ₹900 in charges. That holds whether you do it at once or in two stages. Hidden long-term losses (about ₹1.0 lakh, mostly in HDFC Bank's 2022 shares, LIC and Wipro's pre-bonus shares) cancel out most of the ₹1.45 lakh of long-term gains. The staged route sells the winners before 31 March 2027 and the losers after 1 April 2027. It changes only one thing: you carry forward about ₹66,000 of losses to use against future gains. None of this needs a market forecast. Each step pays off through lower fees, the tax rules or fixed risk limits, which is why the earlier research ranked these steps "near-certain" ([earlier research](file:///home/user/Portfolio/reports/Better%20than%20coin%20flip%20decisions.md)). The split used below, **60% index / 15% stocks / 15% debt / 10% gold**, is an **example for you to accept or change**, not a recommendation. This report is general information, **not tax advice**. Check the tax points with a chartered accountant (CA) or your fund house's tax guide before you act.

## The one-page checklist: what to do, in order

Words used below: an **index fund** is a mutual fund that simply copies a market index such as the Nifty 50. A **Direct plan** is the version with no distributor commission, so it charges a lower fee. **TER** (total expense ratio) is that yearly fee, taken out of the fund automatically. A **SIP** (systematic investment plan) is an automatic monthly purchase. **LTCG/STCG** are long-term and short-term capital gains (shares held more than 12 months, or 12 months or less). **FIFO** (first in, first out) means that when you sell part of a holding, the tax law treats the oldest shares as sold first.

1. **This week:** confirm you have an emergency fund of about six months' expenses in a savings account, a bank FD or a liquid fund, kept **outside** this portfolio. If you don't, build it before anything below.
2. **This week:** write down your split. The example here is 60% index core, 15% individual stocks (at most 8 stocks, at most 5% each), 15% debt, 10% gold. If you want a smaller worst-case fall, use the rule in the split section to choose your own.
3. **This week:** in your broker's mutual-fund section, start a monthly SIP into a **Nifty 50 index fund – Direct Plan – Growth** (the default pick is below). Until the debt share reaches its target, send each month's new savings to a **debt fund** instead (see step 3 of the calendar).
4. **Now to December 2026:** sell the "winner" positions listed in tranche 1 of the transition section. Within the same week, put the money into the index fund. Don't leave it sitting in cash.
5. **After 27 December 2026:** sell ONGC, once its last lots have been held for more than 12 months.
6. **January to mid-March 2027:** run `tax_planner.py`. If any of the ₹1.25 lakh tax-free allowance is left, sell the kept stocks that show a gain (LGELEC, NTPC, DRREDD) and buy them back the next trading day. This raises their purchase price at zero tax. Finish all selling by about **20 March**.
7. **From 1 April 2027:** sell the "loser" positions in tranche 2 (LIC, the HDFC Bank trim, Wipro and the others), plus GAIL and the silver trim. Use the money to finish filling the index core and the debt fund.
8. **FY 2027-28 (April 2027 to March 2028):** don't sell other winners or harvest gains this year. They would use up the carried-forward loss.
9. **By 31 July each year:** file your income-tax return on time, with the capital-gains schedules. A late return loses the right to carry losses forward.
10. **Every April and October:** compare each part of the portfolio with its target. Act only if one has drifted by more than about 5 percentage points (the calendar section explains how).
11. **Never:** buy the Regular plan, choose the IDCW (payout) option, use a market order on an ETF, or sell and rebuy the same share on the same day.

## One fund answers the "ETF, Nifty 50" question

The earlier advice ("ETF, Nifty 50") mixed up two separate choices. The first is **what** to own: the Nifty 50, India's 50 largest companies. The second is **how** to own it: an index fund or an ETF. An **ETF** (exchange-traded fund) holds the same basket of shares, but it trades on NSE like a share. You buy it through your demat account (the electronic account that holds your shares), in whole units, at a live price that can drift slightly above or below its fair value. That fair value is called **iNAV**, published every 15 seconds ([WIRC-ICAI SEBI update](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/law-updates/sebi-aug-2022.pdf)). An index fund is bought from the fund house at that day's closing price (**NAV**, net asset value). You pay in rupee amounts, fractions of units are allowed, there's no brokerage or depository charge, and SIPs start at ₹100 ([Kuvera](https://kuvera.in/mutual-funds/fund/navi-nifty-50-index-fund-direct-plan-growth--ESNIGD-GR)). ETFs are cheaper to hold: about **0.03–0.05%** a year for the big Nifty 50 ETFs ([sharpely](https://sharpely.in/etfs/compare/nippon-india-nifty-bees-vs-sbi-nifty-50-etf)), against about **0.13–0.20%** for good index funds. On your planned ₹4.05 lakh core, that gap is roughly **₹300–600 a year**. A single bad ETF purchase at a premium, a depository charge of about ₹20 on every sale ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/help-center/257381)), or the effort of placing limit orders every month can easily cost that much. **For you, the index fund wins**, because your plan involves monthly SIPs, twice-yearly rebalancing and yearly gain harvesting, and all three are easier to do in exact rupee amounts.

**Why the Nifty 50 rather than the Nifty 500:** the Nifty 50 has the cheapest funds and the longest record. The Nifty 500 adds mid- and small-cap companies and covers about 92% of the market, but its worst historical fall was about the same (−64% against −60%) and its funds cost a little more ([Basunivesh](https://www.basunivesh.com/nifty-50-vs-nifty-500-index-fund-which-one-should-you-pick/)). Either is a sound core. **Pick one and never hold both.** One thing to watch: HDFC Bank is about **10%** of the Nifty 50 ([Taurus MF constituents, Aug 2026](https://www.taurusmutualfund.com/sites/default/files/2026-08/nifty-index-constituent-as-on-19_08_2026.pdf)), and Reliance is also a large weight. Your example stock list keeps both as well. Together with the index, your HDFC Bank exposure would be about ₹74,000, or about 11% of the portfolio. That is acceptable, but don't add more on top.

## One rule sets the debt and gold split

**The rule: equity share ≈ the worst portfolio fall you could sit through without selling, divided by 50%.** Gold gets 10%, and debt gets the rest. The 50% figure comes from Indian shares having fallen about 50–60% from peak to bottom: the worst fall in 20 years of FundsIndia data was −59.5%. In the same data, a 70/15/15 equity/debt/gold mix fell at most −29.2% and still returned **12.3%** a year, against 11% for all-equity ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/can-adding-gold-and-debt-to-equities-boost-long-term-returns-while-reducing-portfolio-drawdowns-561126-2026-10-13)). The repo's own test on Nifty data from 2017 to 2026 found that moving 20% into debt cut the worst fall from about −36% to −30%. That happened in every one of the test's simulated replays (bootstrap resamples), at a cost of about 0.7 percentage points of yearly return ([earlier research](file:///home/user/Portfolio/reports/Loss%20control%20beyond%20stocks.md)). Pick your row once. Change it only when your life changes (job, family, when you'll need the money), never after a market move. Money you need within three years doesn't belong in this split at all.

**Worked example on your ₹6,75,074.** The example target has 75% in equity (60% index plus 15% stocks), which matches a tolerable fall of about 37%. That puts ₹5,06,306 in equity, ₹1,01,261 in debt and ₹67,507 in gold. Now take a crash in which equity falls 50%, gold stays flat and debt earns 3%. Equity loses ₹2,53,153 and debt gains ₹3,038, so the portfolio drops to about **₹4,24,960, a fall of 37%**. Your current portfolio is 87.6% equity, with silver standing in for gold. The same crash would cost it about **44%**, and probably more, because silver tends to fall with shares. If 37% feels like too much, the rule gives 60% equity, 30% debt and 10% gold for a fall of about 30%.

**Which debt fund, decided by your tax slab.** Gains on debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at your income-tax slab rate, however long you hold them ([ICICI Pru Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.icicipruamc.com/blob/knowledgecentre/tax_reckoner/Tax%20Reckoner%202026-27.pdf)). **If your slab is 20% or less**, use a **liquid or money-market fund** (Direct – Growth). These hold very short-term loans, can be cashed out the next working day, and are taxed only when you sell. **If your slab is 30%**, use an **arbitrage fund** instead. These earn a debt-like return from small price gaps between markets but are taxed like equity: 12.5% after 12 months. In one worked example that left about 6.1% after tax, against 4.9% for a liquid fund ([Jainam](https://www.jainam.in/blog/arbitrage-fund-vs-liquid-fund/)). Avoid long-term government-bond (gilt) funds as your cushion. The RBI raised its policy rate to **5.50% on 7 October 2026** ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/10/09/rbi-hikes-repo-rate-to-5-50-percent-october-2026/)), and a 10-year gilt fund loses about 7% for every 1 point rise in interest rates. If you have EPF, PPF or FDs, **count them as part of the debt share**. PPF pays 7.1% tax-free ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/small-savings-scheme-interest-rates-unchanged-for-q3-fy27-check-ppf-scss-ssy-rates-558892-2026-09-30)). Still keep some debt in a fund you can sell, so you have money to rebalance with after a crash.

**Gold, not silver.** Gold is a separate risky asset that helps because its falls rarely coincide with share-market falls. It rose while the Nifty fell in late 2024 and early 2025, yet it dropped **26%** in the first quarter of 2026 ([earlier research](file:///home/user/Portfolio/reports/Loss%20control%20beyond%20stocks.md)). Hold it through a low-cost gold ETF, which you already own (ICIGOL). Sovereign gold bonds bought on the exchange lost their tax-free maturity benefit from April 2026 ([Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/opinion-budget-curtails-tax-free-status-of-sovereign-gold-bonds)), so buy those only below their gold value. Silver depends heavily on industrial demand and doesn't protect you in a crash. Today your ₹83,528 "gold" share is **60% silver** (ICIPSE). The example trims silver so that gold and silver together make up 10%. Do the trim only after the silver lots pass 12 months (see the transition timing). Over time, move new gold purchases into gold rather than silver.

## A calendar replaces forecasts: monthly, April, October and January to March

Vanguard's long-run test found that checking once or twice a year and acting only when an asset drifts more than about 5 points from target kept risk close to the target with very few trades. Its 60/40 example had about 36 rebalances over the whole test period. A portfolio left alone drifted to 81% shares, with volatility of 13.2% against 9.8% ([Vanguard 2010](https://indexacapital.com/bundles/unaiadvisor/docs/papers/2010-Vanguard-Best-practices-for-portfolio-rebalancing.pdf?v=241); [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/investment/art-rebalancing)). Fewer trades in India also mean less tax. For the example target, the **bands** (the allowed drift range) are: equity 70–80%, debt 10–20%, gold 7.5–12.5%, no single stock above about 6%, and stocks in total no more than about 20%. With a portfolio under ₹10 lakh, new savings usually fix any drift faster than markets cause it. So skip any rebalancing trade under ₹5,000.

| When | What to do (about 5–30 minutes) |
|---|---|
| **Every month (SIP day)** | Write down each part's value. Send the whole month's new money to the part furthest **below** target. Sell nothing. Every purchase is a separate "lot" with its own 12-month tax clock. |
| **April** | Check the bands. If everything is inside them, stop. If something is outside and 3–6 months of new money would fix it, redirect your SIPs. If not, sell the overweight part back to target. Sell in this order: holdings with long-term gains that fit within the ₹1.25 lakh allowance, then holdings at a loss, then anything past its exit-load period. Avoid lots held 12 months or less (20% tax). April is also when you plan the year's harvesting. |
| **October** | Same band check as April. Also note any lots that will cross 12 months before March. |
| **Any time: crash rule** | If equity falls below 70% (for example after a 25–30% market fall), sell debt or gold and buy the index fund back to target **within a week**. In a worked ₹10 lakh example, the tax on this was only about ₹1,500 ([repo note](file:///home/user/Portfolio/research_notes/Near%20certain%20investing%20playbook/rebalancing_tax_mechanics.md)). Write this rule down in advance; acting on it is the hard part. |
| **January to 20 March** | Run `tax_planner.py`. **Loss harvesting** (selling a holding at a loss to cancel out gains): if you have already booked taxable gains this year, sell loss-making holdings to offset them. **Gain harvesting**: sell enough long-term units to book gains up to ₹1,25,000 minus any long-term gains already booked this year, then buy back **the next trading day**, not the same day ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/tax-loss-harvesting-related/128956)). This saves up to **₹15,625 plus cess per person per year** ([Kuvera](https://kuvera.in/blog/save-up-to-%E2%82%B910000-in-ltcg-taxes-with-tax-harvesting/amp/)). Check every lot's purchase date before selling in March. |
| **By 31 July** | File your return on time (ITR-2 or ITR-3, with the capital-gains and carried-forward-loss schedules). A late return gives up carried-forward losses ([Kanakkupillai](https://www.kanakkupillai.com/learn/how-to-set-off-and-carry-forward-capital-losses/)). |

Four rules sit behind the tax steps. Short-term gains on shares and equity funds are taxed at 20%. Long-term gains are taxed at 12.5% on the amount above ₹1.25 lakh a year ([Tata MF](https://www.tatamutualfund.com/blogs/post-budget-2026-new-tax-map-equity-investors)). A short-term loss can offset any gain, but a long-term loss can offset only long-term gains. Unused losses carry forward for 8 years if you file on time. Two consequences follow. First, booking a loss in a year when your gains are already within ₹1.25 lakh **wastes** the loss, because it cancels gains that were tax-free anyway. Second, because of FIFO, you can't pick an expensive recent lot to sell for a loss while older, cheaper shares of the same stock sit ahead of it in the same demat account ([CBDT Circular 768](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/768-circular-no.-768-dated-24-6-1998-1)). Don't harvest debt funds. They're taxed at your slab rate whenever you sell, so there's nothing to save.

## What is not certain

Five things in this plan are not confirmed. **First, two holdings don't reconcile.** Your trade records plus corporate actions give 101 RELIND shares, but the broker shows 76, so **25 shares are unaccounted for**. If there was an unrecorded sale, an earlier year had a realised gain. On the worst-case cost assumption, the RELIND trim adds about ₹22,000 of gain, which still fits inside the allowance. LIC shows 80 shares, against 45 in the trade records. The 35-share gap is assumed to be the May 2022 IPO allotment, at about ₹902 per share. The IPO dates for TATCAP, LGELEC and NTPGRE are also assumed. If LGELEC turned out to be short-term, its harvest would cost about ₹1,000 in tax. Check all of these against your CAS and IPO allotment notes. **Second, the tax section numbers are not verified.** The new Income-tax Act 2025 sections (196 and 198, replacing 111A and 112A) come from secondary sources ([Ebizfiling](https://ebizfiling.com/blog/section-196-and-198-short-term-and-long-term-capital-gains/)). So do three working assumptions: that you can choose the order in which losses offset gains, that FIFO circulars still apply under the new Act, and that the ITR due date is 31 July. Neither the statute nor an official tax guide could be opened during research. **Third, prices move the lists.** At 15 October prices the portfolio was 7.6% lower, and JSWENE, JIOFIN, ONGC, HINPET and ICIPSE had moved from gains to losses, so re-run the calculator before each tranche. **Fourth, fees are snapshots.** Most TERs come from websites that collect fund data, not from the fund houses' own factsheets, and Navi's 0.06% has no 2026 confirmation. **Fifth, the calculations assume no gains elsewhere.** They assume you booked no other gains this year (in another demat account or in mutual funds), and that your slab is 30% for the silver calculation.

## Conclusion

Your portfolio's gains and losses largely cancel out. The broker shows +₹37,723 in total, but underneath sit ₹1.45 lakh of long-term gains and ₹1.0 lakh of long-term losses. Bonus shares created those offsetting amounts, and FIFO decides which ones you realise when you sell. That is why a complete overhaul costs nothing in tax. It also shows the risk of trimming by looking only at the broker screen: an HDFC Bank trim that looks like a profit is actually a loss for tax. After the move, the gains come from the structure: lower fees, a fixed debt-and-gold cushion, a yearly tax-free harvest, and a calendar that tells you when to act.

The plan doesn't tell you how much risk to take. That is the one decision left to you. Pick the worst fall you could sit through, divide it by 50% to get your equity share, and write the split down before the next crash. Then automate the rest. The two-tranche schedule is only worth up to ₹8,600, so if it starts to feel complicated, selling everything now is a perfectly good choice.
