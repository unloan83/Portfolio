# Build a selection engine, not more rules

Turning this engine into an alpha engine is a change of job, not a matter of tuning. Today it is a **risk monitor**. Its rules decide when to trim or review stocks you already own. They never decide *which* stocks to own, so the engine has no source of excess return. The hindcast confirms this: following its decisions earned **3.70%/yr less than doing nothing** (Oct 2022–Oct 2025, CI −9.26% to +0.84%) for a smaller drawdown (−20.9% vs −26.3%), and its REVIEW/ACCUMULATE scores had an information coefficient of **−0.02 (t −0.25)**, which is no predictive power at all ([hindcast_summary.md](file:///home/user/Portfolio/reports/hindcast_summary.md)). To beat the market you need three things the repo lacks: a ranking signal with documented out-of-sample evidence (in India that means intermediate-term momentum, ideally blended with low volatility or quality), a survivorship-free universe of about 200–500 stocks to rank, and a validation process strict enough to tell a real edge from a lucky backtest. A realistic long-run payoff is **+1 to +3 percentage points a year on the factor sleeve after costs and tax**, earned in bursts and paid for with multi-year stretches of underperformance. The cheapest way to get most of it is a factor index fund. A DIY engine has to beat that fund after tax before it earns its keep. This report is research, not personalised financial advice.

## The current engine manages risk; it was never built to pick winners

Most "improve the engine" ideas fail for a structural reason. Every rule in `signal_engine.py` (trailing trims, 200-DMA reviews, accumulate gates, weight caps) acts on a fixed list of 34 holdings. A rule that only removes or trims names can lower drawdown, but it can raise return only if it reliably exits losers before they lag. The hindcast shows it does the reverse. Names flagged REVIEW went on to *beat* NIFTYBEES by an average of **13.6%** over the next year (NW t −3.96, verdict "out of line"). ACCUMULATE names were no better than chance (59.5% hit rate, bootstrap CI on excess return −4.4% to +9.2%) ([hindcast_summary.md](file:///home/user/Portfolio/reports/hindcast_summary.md)). The external evidence says the same about market-level trend exits. Kuvera found the common 200-day moving-average crossover "does not consistently beat a buy-and-hold strategy in the U.S. or India," and costs and taxes make it worse ([Kuvera](https://kuvera.in/blog/promises-and-pitfalls-of-simple-moving-average-strategies/)). Its value is defence: it has helped mainly in recessions and falling markets ([Quantified Strategies](https://quantifiedstrategies.substack.com/p/200-day-ma-strategy-backtest-rules-1bb)).

So keep the current engine for what it does well: limiting drawdowns, enforcing caps and catching data errors. Do not expect another round of threshold tuning to produce alpha. The hindcast's own tuning step reached that conclusion. The best of 14 configurations had a deflated Sharpe of 0.18 and a probability of backtest overfitting of 0.45, and it failed its out-of-sample test ([hindcast_summary.md](file:///home/user/Portfolio/reports/hindcast_summary.md)).

**Data bug that affects the earlier results.** `data/prices/adj/NIFTYBEES.NS.csv` holds unadjusted closes on **2019-12-19 and 2019-12-20 (≈13.02 vs ≈130)**, left over from the 1:10 unit split. The repo loader does not catch them. Untreated, they create a fake −90% benchmark drawdown, pull the 200-DMA down for about 200 sessions, and corrupt any 252-day excess return that starts or ends on those dates ([NIFTYBEES.NS.csv](file:///home/user/Portfolio/data/prices/adj/NIFTYBEES.NS.csv); [internal_signal_tests.md](file:///home/user/Portfolio/research_notes/Excess%20return%20engine%20design/internal_signal_tests.md)). **Update: fixed and re-run.** The loader now drops isolated bad prints (`remove_price_spikes` in `hindcast/data.py`), and the hindcast was re-run on the cleaned benchmark. The conclusions are unchanged: the primary window is identical, the TRAIN pick is still `trail_trim_half`, DSR is still 0.18, PBO moved from 0.45 to 0.50, and the recommendation is still to keep current parameters.

## Momentum is India's strongest premium, and its most fragile

**Hypothesis.** Stocks that have outperformed over the past 6–12 months, skipping the most recent month, keep outperforming for the next 3–12 months. **Mechanism.** Investors under-react to news and then herd. Index and fund flows reinforce the trend.

**External evidence.** It is the best-documented factor in India. Survivorship-corrected IIMA factor data for 1993–2013 show momentum as the standout long-short premium ([IIMA WP](https://www.iima.ac.in/publication/four-factor-model-indian-equities-market)). Long-only tests on the top 100–200 stocks find that momentum, low-vol and quality alpha survive realistic trading costs ([Raju & Teli, SSRN 4000418](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4000418)). NSE reports **19.19%/yr for Nifty200 Momentum 30 against 15.08% for Nifty 200**, about +4.1 pp/yr from 2005 to 2026. The index launched in 2020, so roughly 15 of those 21 years are back-test ([NSE whitepaper 2026](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty200-momentum-30/momentum-strategy-whitepaper_2026.pdf)).

**Failure modes.** Momentum crashes are severe. A third-party net-of-cost back-test shows a **−70.5% drawdown in 2008 and a ~65-month recovery** ([BacktestIndia](https://backtestindia.com/blog/momentum-investing-india-backtest)). From 27 Sep 2024 to 7 Apr 2025 the momentum index fell **31.8% against 15.4% for the Nifty 50** ([Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/blog/momentum-mutual-funds-in-bull-bear-markets/)). Live funds were still below their peak in August 2026, with 1-year returns of 0.7–1.8% against a category average of 7–8% ([Arthgyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/fund/kotak-nifty-200-momentum-30-index-fund-direct-growth/151781)). The index's 5-year CAGR is **9.28% against 15.98% over 10 years** ([Bandhan](https://bandhanmutual.com/mutual-funds/index-funds-and-etfs/bandhan-nifty200-momentum-30-index-fund/regular)). Crashes cluster in panic states, when markets rebound after a fall in high volatility ([Daniel & Moskowitz](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20439/w20439.pdf)). Crowding is now material too. The December 2025 rebalance replaced 19 of 30 names and moved about ₹16k crore, with flows exceeding a day's volume in 27 stocks ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/nifty200-momentum30-a-flip-of-index-switch-jolts-16k-cr-across-the-grid-125121400388_1.html)).

**Low volatility and quality are the companions, not the engine.** In a net back-test, low-vol cut the worst drawdown to −44.6% from the Nifty's −55.1% ([BacktestIndia low-vol](https://backtestindia.com/blog/low-volatility-anomaly-india-nse-backtest)). Quality, low-vol and multi-factor indices fell less than the benchmark in the three largest drawdowns ([S&P DJI](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/research/research-an-index-approach-to-factor-investing-in-india.pdf)). Their excess *return* over the market is small and regime-dependent. Quality lagged value by about 30 pp in 2021 ([UTI NFO](https://static.smartspends.com/static/docs/nfo/nfo-uti-nifty200-quality-30-index-fund.pdf)). Their value lies in having bad years at different times from momentum. That makes the blend easier to hold, which matters more than a fraction of a point of return. Factor timing is not worth attempting: value-spread timing adds little after costs compared with a fixed allocation ([AQR](https://www.Aqr.Com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/Contrarian-Factor-Timing-is-Deceptively-Difficult-Supplement)). PEAD, delivery volume and size have weak or mixed Indian evidence. They are not engine material.

## Your own 34 names confirm the direction, not the size

Internal tests on the repo's price cache (point-in-time, next-day execution, 0.25% one-way cost, July 2018 to October 2026) line up with the literature on direction but cannot pin down the size of the edge ([internal_signal_tests.md](file:///home/user/Portfolio/research_notes/Excess%20return%20engine%20design/internal_signal_tests.md)).

**6-1 momentum was the only signal with a meaningful rank IC.** It scored **+0.088 at 3 months (NW t 2.73) and +0.090 at 6 months (t 2.49)**. Low volatility, 1-month reversal and 52-week-high proximity showed nothing usable. All four 6-1 momentum books ranked at the 99th–100th percentile of 200 random-selection books of the same size and buffer. The best one, top 15 held until it drops out of the top 20, beat equal-weighting the 34 names by **+3.7%/yr (95% CI +0.5 to +7.3)**.

Three caveats cut that number down. First, it is the **best of 24 configurations**, and a multiple-testing adjustment would very likely push the lower bound below zero. Second, the edge is a **2018–22 phenomenon**. The 3-month IC was +0.162 (t 6.1) then and **−0.009 in 2023–26**. Calendar 2021 alone contributed about 24 points, mostly a PSU, power and energy rotation in a portfolio that holds 6 power and 6 oil-and-gas names. Third, the period an engine built today would actually have lived through (2023–26) shows no significant IC for any signal, and 2025–26 was negative for the momentum books.

The headline "your portfolio beats the index" result is mostly an illusion. Equal-weighting the 34 names beat NIFTYBEES by **+6.7%/yr**, but those are today's survivors, chosen partly with hindsight. That gap measures the selection bias in the universe, not skill you can repeat.

Two findings do generalise, because both match the external evidence. A **rank buffer** cut momentum turnover from about 330% to about 130% a year with no consistent loss of edge, as Novy-Marx & Velikov predict ([NBER w20721](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20721.pdf)). **Lookback length drives crash exposure.** In the Sep 2024–Mar 2025 sell-off, 12-1 momentum (top 10) lost **31.8%**, 6-1 momentum lost 14.3%, and equal weight lost 16.6%. The 12-month window was still full of the 2023–24 PSU winners. Volatility-adjusted 12-1 momentum lost 19.1% and was the only signal with positive IC in both sub-periods.

**Parameter sensitivity, in plain terms:** the edge moves by 2–4 points a year depending on lookback (6 vs 12 months), book size (10 vs 15) and buffer. That is as large as the edge itself. Any DIY design must be chosen *before* testing and judged as a family, not by its best member.

## Three realistic routes, ranked by cost per unit of evidence

Costs and taxes decide between the options more than the signals do. Delivery trades cost about **0.22% round trip** at a zero-brokerage broker, made up of STT at 0.1% each side, stamp duty and exchange fees ([Zerodha charges](https://zerodha.com/charges)). Short-term gains are taxed at **20%**, and long-term gains above ₹1.25 lakh a year at **12.5%** ([Income Tax Dept s.196](https://www.incometaxindia.gov.in/w/section-196-78); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/long-term-capital-gains-on-shares)). Momentum sells most winners within 12 months, so a DIY sleeve realises STCG every year. A mutual fund turns over inside the scheme and the unitholder pays nothing until redemption. A practitioner estimate puts the drag from monthly momentum at about "4% tax costs on 1% gains" at the old 15% STCG rate ([Raju](https://x.com/rajanraju26/status/1859091783231177157)). Direct-plan momentum index funds charge about **0.20–0.37% TER** with tracking error around 0.2–0.5% ([Kotak factsheet](https://www.kotakmf.com/factsheet/August_2026/kotak/NIFTY-200-MOMENTUM-30-INDEX-FUND.html); [Baroda BNP](https://www.barodabnpparibasmf.in/efactsheet/Jun2026/Innerpages/Nifty200-Momentum-30-Index-Fund.html)). The fund's hidden cost is rebalance-day impact. That impact is a DIY sleeve's one structural advantage, because a ₹20–30k ticket traded off the June/December dates avoids it.

The honest whole-portfolio arithmetic is sobering. A 40% satellite earning +2 pp adds **+0.8 pp/yr** to the total, in a good decade. Option A captures most of that with almost no tax drag or engineering, so it is the benchmark every DIY design must beat. Option B is worth building only for its learning value or if a preregistered test shows a clear margin over the fund after tax. A DIY sleeve is also the only route that turns the repo into a real selection engine, and that is the question you asked.

## A gated roadmap that can say "no" cheaply

Each stage has a pass/fail gate decided in advance. Failing a gate means falling back to option A, not tuning until it passes. Much of the hindcast harness is reusable:

- `hindcast/stats.py` already implements Newey-West t-statistics, block-bootstrap CIs, `deflated_sharpe`, `pbo_cscv` (combinatorially symmetric cross-validation, CSCV) and `purge_train`.
- `hindcast/run.py` provides the offline replay, the shadow books (follow-model, do-nothing, benchmark), regime labels and the acceptance-check table.
- `data/hindcast/grid.json` is the preregistration pattern.
- `hindcast/data.py` provides corporate-action repair and ticker stitching, with sha256-pinned manifests.

([hindcast/stats.py](file:///home/user/Portfolio/hindcast/stats.py); [hindcast/run.py](file:///home/user/Portfolio/hindcast/run.py)). Three things are new: the universe, the ranking and portfolio construction, and a cost-plus-tax model. The DSR/PBO thresholds follow Bailey & López de Prado ([SSRN 2460551](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2460551); [SSRN 2326253](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)).

Large unexplained divergence = bug; stop and fix |

Report every trial. | 2–4 weeks | Net-of-tax excess over the **momentum fund NAV** > +1.5 pp/yr. DSR > 0.95, PBO < 0.2. Positive in both halves (pre-2019, 2020+) and in the 2024–25 crash window. Adjacent configs agree in sign. Otherwise use option A |

Converge over 2–4 cycles. | Ongoing | **Kill switch:** trails the fund by >1.5 pp/yr over 36 months, costs or tax run >50% over model, or an implementation error recurs, then move the sleeve into the fund. Two cautions apply to the gates. First, Stage 2 will probably be harder to pass than it looks. The internal 2023–26 IC was zero, and the live momentum index has lagged since late 2024. A **"no" at Stage 2 is a valid and useful answer.** Second, an Indian momentum sleeve should be expected to lose to the market for two consecutive years at some point ([Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/learn/equity-funds/momentum-vs-value-factor-investing-2026/)). Writing down the abandonment rule now is what stops you quitting at the bottom.

## Conclusion

The question was never which risk rule to tune. The engine's REVIEW signal pointed the wrong way, and a one-line momentum rank on the same names did better than every rule in it. The deeper lesson from the 34-name tests is that **the universe matters more than the signal**. Holding the names equal-weight explained more return than any selection, and that return came from hindsight. An alpha engine therefore stands or falls on Stage 1, the survivorship-free data build. Signal cleverness comes second. The practical path is to adopt option A as the hurdle now, build the data and test once against preregistered gates, and let a DIY sleeve earn its place by beating a 0.2%-TER index fund after tax.

**Decisions you need to make:** (1) Should the engine stay a risk monitor with a factor-fund satellite (option A), or should you invest the 3–6 months to attempt a selection engine (options B/C)? (2) What satellite weight, 25% or 40%, and what momentum vs low-vol/quality split? (3) Can you accept a written rule not to abandon the factor sleeve over a drawdown shorter than about five years? (4) Should rebalancing be quarterly (fresher signal, more STCG) or semi-annual (less tax)? (5) Free-but-manual NSE bhavcopy, or a paid vendor such as EODHD at about $20/month for delisted history ([EODHD](https://eodhd.com/financial-apis/review-delisted-stock-companies-data))? (6) How should the transition of the current 34 holdings respect your existing STCG lots?
