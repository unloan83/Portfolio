# Win on costs, tax and time, not forecasts

For an Indian investor, the decisions that reliably beat a coin flip are almost all **arithmetic or structural, not predictive**. Examples: choosing direct over regular plans, index over active large-cap funds, holding gains past 12 months, harvesting the ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption, diversifying beyond a handful of stocks, keeping a fixed debt share and rebalancing, staying out of F&O and intraday trading, and holding equity for seven years or more. Each of these has a measured or arithmetic probability of helping of roughly **75% to ~100%**, and none needs a view on the market. Among market signals, only one is strongly forecastable: **risk**. In this repo's Nifty data, a high-volatility month was followed by another above-median month **68–75%** of the time, against 50% by chance. Long-horizon **valuation** shifts the *level* of 5–7-year returns, but not their sign. **Direction** over days, months or a year is close to a coin flip: 54% of days were up, and monthly return autocorrelation was −0.04. Retail F&O, analyst targets, recession calls, single-stock stop-losses and fast trend switches have done **worse than a coin flip**. Two warnings apply to everything below. The repo-measured numbers come from **one 9.4-year path (June 2017 to October 2026)** that contains one crash and no slow bear market. And many external figures were read from search-engine extracts because the source sites were blocked, so treat them as reported, not independently checked. This is general research, not personalised advice.

"Arithmetic" means the benefit follows from costs or tax rules and needs no forecast. ## Seven decisions win without predicting anything

The top of the ladder is dominated by decisions where the payoff is guaranteed by rules, not by markets. **Direct plans** hold the same portfolio as regular plans at a lower expense ratio. The typical equity gap is about **0.95 pp** (≈1.12% vs ≈2.07% TER) ([Kuvera](https://kuvera.in/blog/?p=42405)), and the reported range is 30–190 bps ([Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/47004/direct-plans-are-hugely-underappreciated/)). At 12% gross over 20 years, a 1 pp gap costs about **16% of terminal wealth**. The tax levers are equally mechanical. Since 23 July 2024, listed equity gains are taxed at **20% if held for 12 months or less** and **12.5% above ₹1.25 lakh a year** if held longer ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/budget-2024-all-the-key-changes-to-capital-gains-tax-explained-124072500330_1.html); [ClearTax](https://cleartax.in/s/long-term-capital-gains-on-shares)). A ₹5 lakh gain costs ₹1,00,000 as STCG and ₹46,875 as LTCG. Annual harvesting of the exemption is worth at most **₹15,625 per person per year**: certain, but small for large portfolios. The absence of a wash-sale rule was not verified against primary law.

**Index over active** is the strongest *non-arithmetic* edge, because it rests on a measured base rate rather than a forecast. Per S&P's year-end 2025 scorecard, **75.0% / 74.2% / 84.4% / 76.3%** of active large-cap funds lagged over 1/3/5/10 years ([SPIVA India YE2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india-year-end-2025/)). Mid/small-cap is the honest exception over short horizons. A majority of those funds *beat* their benchmark in 2025, though about **82%** still lagged over 10 years to mid-2025 ([SPIVA India Mid-Year 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-india-scorecard-mid-year-2025.pdf)). Picking last period's winner does not help. Not one US top-quartile fund at December 2020 stayed top-quartile in each of the next four years ([S&P Persistence](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/us-persistence-scorecard)). These figures came from search extracts of S&P pages and should be checked against the PDFs.

**Diversification is near-certain for risk and only modestly likely for return.** In the repo, a stock beat NIFTYBEES over its full history only **44%** of the time (12 of 27). Single stocks **lost money in 17.7% of 3-year windows, against 0% for Nifty**. The median stock's maximum drawdown was **−64%, against −36%** ([repo analysis](file:///home/user/Portfolio/research_notes/Better%20than%20coin%20flip%20decisions/repo_measured_probabilities.md)). Random equal-weight baskets show where the risk goes. Moving from 1 to 5 names took the worst-decile drawdown from −80% to −57% and narrowed the CAGR spread from 17 pp to 7 pp, with little further gain beyond 10 names. That universe is survivor-biased and hand-picked, so the true underperformance rate is worse. Globally, **57.4% of non-US stocks** trailed one-month T-bills over their lifetimes over 1990–2020, and **2.4% of firms** created all net wealth ([Bessembinder et al., ASU](https://wpcarey.asu.edu/department-finance/faculty-research/do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills)).

**Allocation is the only loss-limiter that worked in every test.** A fixed 80/20 equity/debt mix cut maximum drawdown from −36% to −30% in every bootstrap resample, for about **0.7 pp of CAGR**. That figure assumes debt earns 6.5%; at 0% cash the cost was about 2 pp ([earlier allocation tests](file:///home/user/Portfolio/research_notes/Loss%20control%20beyond%20stocks/internal_allocation_tests.md)). Rebalancing keeps risk on target with mechanical certainty. Its return effect is uncertain, roughly 0–50 bps a year in Vanguard's estimates ([Portfolio Adviser](https://portfolio-adviser.com/?p=6042)).

**Behaviour and holding period** complete the set. Investors earned **13.2%** a year against **18.7%** for the funds they held over 2003–2020 ([Business Standard on Axis MF](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pf/returns-made-by-average-mf-investor-lag-those-of-schemes-and-sips-study-121040101236_1.html)). Morningstar India measured the gap at **2.5–5.8 pp a year**, mainly from badly timed entries and exits ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pf/reduce-behaviour-gap-with-higher-exposure-to-diversified-funds-122102600668_1.html)). Holding period changes the odds dramatically. Over June 1999 to September 2026, Nifty 50 TRI lost money in **24% of 1-year, 6% of 3-year, 0.1% of 5-year and 0% of 7-year-plus windows**. The worst 10-year CAGR was still about **5%**, below FD rates ([FundsIndia Oct 2026](https://fundsindia.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/202610-FundsIndia-Wealth-Conversations.pdf)). The column mapping of that table could not be confirmed from the PDF. The repo path agrees in direction: **85.7%** of 1-year windows were positive, but with a realistic CI of **55–96%**. Every 3- and 5-year window was positive, yet those rest on only about 3 and 2 independent windows, so the honest lower bound is roughly 30–50%, not 99%.

Finally, **SEBI's own data** make abstention a high-probability decision. **91.0%** of individual F&O traders lost money in FY25, with net losses of ₹1,05,603 crore ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)). Over **70%** of intraday traders lost money in FY23, and loss-makers paid costs equal to a further 57% of their losses ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html)).

## Risk is forecastable, valuation tilts the decade, direction is a coin flip

Signals split cleanly into three groups. **Volatility clusters.** In the repo, monthly realised volatility correlated **0.57** with the next month's (CI 0.37–0.71; R² 0.37 on log-vol). After an above-median-vol month, the next was also above median **67.9%** of the time, or **74.5%** using a median known in real time, against 50% by chance. The return series shows nothing comparable: R² was **0.002** and monthly autocorrelation **−0.04** ([repo analysis](file:///home/user/Portfolio/research_notes/Better%20than%20coin%20flip%20decisions/repo_measured_probabilities.md)). Indian studies agree that GARCH models, especially when augmented with India VIX, forecast Nifty realised volatility well ([IIM Calcutta WPS 797](https://studentlive.iimcal.ac.in/sites/all/files/pdfs/wps_797.pdf)). Use volatility to size positions and set expectations, not to pick direction. Volatility-*targeting* as a return strategy failed real-time tests: out-of-sample versions underperformed in **72 of 103** cases ([Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)).

**Valuation shifts expected 5–7-year returns, not their sign.** Days in the cheapest fifth of Nifty P/E (<17.3) had a median 5-year return of **+23.6%**, against **+13.4%** for the most expensive fifth (>23.9). Yet worst-case 3-year returns were negative in *every* band ([indexPe](https://indexpe.in/pe-vs-forward-returns)). NSE switched to consolidated earnings in April 2021, so today's P/E is not comparable with that history ([Capitalmind](https://premium.capitalmind.in/nifty-pe-ratio/)). Globally, most published return predictors deliver negative or barely positive out-of-sample R² ([Goyal, Welch & Zafirov via SFI](https://sfi.ch/en/publications/a-comprehensive-2022-look-at-the-empirical-performance-of-equity-premium-prediction)). The defensible use is a modest tilt in long-run allocation, not exits.

**Direction is a coin flip, and the 200-DMA shows why on this path.** Below its 200-day average, Nifty went on to rise over the next 63 days **85%** of the time, with a mean of +7.3%. Above the average, it rose **62%** of the time, with a mean of +2.0%. A drawdown of ≥5% in the next quarter was equally likely in both states (56–60%). Most below-average spells here were V-shaped dips: 15 of the 20 best days occurred below the 200-DMA. A daily switch cut maximum drawdown from **−36% to −17%** but cost about **4 pp of CAGR** (6.9% vs 11.0%; 8.3% if cash earns 6%) over 73 switches, before tax. The earlier monthly 10-month filter looked better: −14% drawdown at 10.7% CAGR with cash earning an assumed 6.5%. That result rests on one lucky month-end exit on 2 March 2020. Five of its seven exits cost money, and across resampled paths its median CAGR cost was **−1.7 pp**, with only a **74%** chance of a shallower drawdown ([earlier allocation tests](file:///home/user/Portfolio/research_notes/Loss%20control%20beyond%20stocks/internal_allocation_tests.md)). The two results agree once read together. Trend rules buy drawdown insurance, and slow bear markets like 2008 repay it ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/Journal-Article/A-Century-of-Evidence-on-Trend-Following-Investing)). This sample contains no such bear market, so the rules paid the premium and collected little. As a *return* signal, trend is Tier 3 or 4. As *risk insurance*, it is plausibly Tier 2, but India's evidence is thin, and moving-average gains were eroded by costs ([Mitra 2011](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v11y2011i2p287-297.html)).

The asymmetry behind "never time the market" is real but one-sided as usually told. Missing the 10 best days cut repo CAGR from **11.0% to 6.0%**, and missing the 10 worst lifted it to 17.6%. But **12 of the 20 best days fell within 10 sessions of a worst day**, and missing both left about 12.3%. Zerodha found the same clustering on the Nifty 500 ([Zerodha Varsity](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/varsity/what-happens-if-you-miss-some-of-the-best-or-worst-days-in-the-market)). The lesson is narrower than the slogan: exits made during high-volatility crashes are what destroy returns.

Factor tilts sit between these groups. Nifty200 Momentum 30 beat Nifty 200 in **98.3%** of rolling 5-year windows, but the index launched in 2020, so this is mostly **backtest** ([NSE whitepaper](https://www.niftyindices.com/docs/default-source/indices/nifty200-momentum-30/nifty200_momentum_30_index_whitepaper_sep_20.pdf)). Live, it fell **31.8% vs 15.4%** for Nifty 50 from September 2024 to April 2025 ([Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/blog/momentum-mutual-funds-in-bull-bear-markets/)). Quality 30 beat the benchmark in 9 of 14 years ([NSE](https://archives.nseindia.com/content/indices/NIFTY200_Quality30_Whitepaper_Final.pdf)). Treat factor tilts as a likely long-horizon premium with multi-year droughts, not a 98% bet.

## Evidence quality: where the confidence is earned and where it is borrowed

The arithmetic rows are as certain as the tax code and expense ratios they rest on. SEBI's trader-loss figures are population data covering crores of accounts. SPIVA and FundsIndia are long, transparent datasets, but both were read from extracts. Several figures are weaker and should be discounted: the FD comparison (inferred, never measured directly), the direct-vs-regular gap (platform estimates, not AMFI), the factor-index hit rates (pre-launch backtests), valuation bands (in-sample, about 1–2 cycles), and Whalesbook's claim that 56% of listed firms trailed a Post Office deposit over three years (methodology unverified, so excluded from the table). Every repo figure describes **one market path** with one V-shaped crash and an unrecovered 2026 drawdown (−12.1% on 15 October 2026). Its single-stock statistics come from a survivor-biased list of current holdings. Repo numbers are best read as a check on direction, not as base rates.

## What this means in practice

Ranked by probability of helping, highest first:

1. Use direct plans and the lowest-cost fund for any index exposure, and check every holding's TER.
2. Never sell an equity gain held for less than 12 months unless forced. Harvest the ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption each year, and book losses against gains.
3. Do not trade F&O or intraday.
4. Hold the equity core in broad index funds rather than active large-cap funds or single stocks. Cap any single stock so that a −64% drawdown in it is survivable.
5. Pick a debt share that matches the drawdown you can sit through, and rebalance with bands, using new cash first.
6. Commit only money with a 7-year-plus horizon to equity, keep an emergency fund, and automate SIPs so a falling market does not stop them.
7. Use volatility to scale expectations and position sizes. Use valuation only as a gentle long-run tilt.

Ignore daily or monthly direction calls, analyst targets, recession forecasts, FII-flow, crude or currency timing, tips, RSI-type technicals, last year's top fund, and stop-losses on single stocks. Treat trend filters as optional, partial insurance, never as a source of return.

The repo can automate the high-probability middle of this list without predicting anything:

- an allocation and rebalancing advisor that checks drift against bands;
- a tax-lot helper that flags lots near 12 months and LTCG headroom;
- a TER and cost check on held funds;
- concentration caps;
- a volatility and drawdown dashboard.

None of these will raise returns by forecasting. Their value is in avoiding certain losses from costs, tax and behaviour.

## Conclusion

The question "which decisions beat a coin flip?" turns out to have an inverted answer. The more a decision depends on predicting the market, the closer it sits to 50%. The more it depends on rules the investor controls (fees, tax timing, diversification, holding period, not trading), the closer it sits to 100%. The one exception is risk itself, which is forecastable enough to size positions but not to time them. The quantitative implication is uncomfortable. The combined certain edges (about 1 pp from costs, 1–6 pp from behaviour, plus tax savings) are larger and more reliable than any signal-based edge documented for India, including momentum's backtested +4 pp. An expert portfolio process should therefore spend its effort on those certain edges first, and treat every predictive rule as a hypothesis that must still clear a preregistered test that includes a 2008-type bear market.
