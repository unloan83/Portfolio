# Buy loss control by holding less equity

No model can make losses zero. A model *can* limit losses, and the evidence is clear about how: every method that reliably makes drawdowns shallower either holds less equity on average or sells after prices have already fallen, so each is **insurance with a premium**, paid in lower return, whipsaw or tax. Every method that claims positive return on top (selling options, trend following, shorting) is harvesting a risk premium or a behavioural edge that can crash. For an Indian investor the cheapest and most robust loss-limiter is a **static multi-asset allocation (equity, debt, gold) with band rebalancing**. In this repo's own test on 2017–2026 Nifty data, moving 20% to debt cut max drawdown by about 7 points in **100% of bootstrap resamples** for about **0.7 percentage points of CAGR**. A slow monthly trend filter is the only overlay with serious global evidence. Here it halved the drawdown, but through one lucky exit, after 13 switches, most of which lost money. Options, shorting and futures trend following are genuine tools with real evidence behind them, but SEBI's **₹15–20 lakh minimum index contract** makes them unusable as hedges below roughly ₹20–50 lakh of capital, and about **91% of individual F&O traders lost money in FY25**. The repo should build an allocation and rebalancing advisor first, add a labelled trend-status signal and a hedge-cost calculator next, and touch F&O only after capital, a preregistered backtest on NSE F&O bhavcopy and a paper-trading period. Much of the external evidence below came from search snippets because many sites were blocked during research, so treat it as needing verification. This is research, not personalised financial advice.

## Every loss-limiter is insurance, and the premium is the equity return you forgo

The useful way to sort these models is by what pays you. **Insurance** (holding debt or gold, buying puts, trend exits, volatility scaling, drawdown stops) costs money in most years and pays out in bear markets. **Risk premia** (selling puts or calls, trend following in futures, long-short factors) earn money in most years and lose it in bursts. Nothing in the evidence is both cheap and reliable. Kaminski and Lo show that under a random walk a stop-loss rule always lowers expected return; it adds value only when returns show momentum at the rule's horizon ([MIT DSpace](https://dspace.mit.edu/entities/publication/bb69ca4b-0cdc-487f-831d-63b2e84fafee)). Index returns show 1–12 month momentum ([Moskowitz, Ooi & Pedersen, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2089463)); single stocks are noisier, which is why the repo's stock-level stops cut drawdown from −26% to −21% but cost **3.70%/yr** against doing nothing ([hindcast_summary.md](file:///home/user/Portfolio/reports/hindcast_summary.md)). Loss control belongs at the portfolio and index level, not the stock level.

## Allocation is the only lever that worked in every test the repo can run

**Hypothesis:** holding a fixed share in assets that do not move with Indian equity cuts drawdown in proportion to that share, whatever the market path. **Mechanism:** arithmetic, plus rebalancing that buys equity after it falls. The repo's tests used cached ETF prices, a constant **6.5% debt accrual (assumed; no debt ETF is cached)**, 0.1% one-way costs and next-day execution ([internal_allocation_tests.md](file:///home/user/Portfolio/research_notes/Loss%20control%20beyond%20stocks/internal_allocation_tests.md)). Window A runs 2017–2026 on Nifty plus debt. Window B adds gold, but only from **December 2023**, because that is where the cached gold ETF starts.

| Window A, 2017-06 to 2026-10 | CAGR | Max DD | Worst 12m | Switches/trades | Bootstrap: P(shallower MaxDD) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nifty buy-and-hold | 11.0% | −36.3% | −28.3% | 0 | — |
| 80/20 equity/debt, 5pp band | 10.4% | −29.6% | −21.7% | 0.3/yr | **1.00** |
| 10-month SMA trend, 1-month signal | 10.7% | **−14.1%** | −10.4% | 13 switches | 0.74–0.91 |
| 10-month SMA, 2-month confirm | 6.2% | −36.3% | −28.7% | 0.75/yr | 0.63 |
| Vol target 15% | 8.8% | −36.3% | −28.5% | 4.2/yr | 0.69 |
| Drawdown rule (−15% → 50% equity) | 8.3% | −28.9% | −21.4% | 1.1/yr | 0.85–0.97 |

The 80/20 mix is the result to rely on. It gave up about **0.7pp CAGR** (bootstrap CI −3.0 to +1.7pp) and was shallower than buy-and-hold in every resample, in every sub-period and under both bootstrap methods. With 0% cash instead of 6.5% debt, the cost rose to about 2pp, so the debt assumption matters. External evidence agrees. Morningstar India found balanced advantage funds' drawdowns tracked their average equity exposure, not their timing skill ([Morningstar India](https://morningstar.in/posts/68775/hybrid-funds-which-protected-the-most-downside.aspx)). Vanguard finds rebalancing frequency barely matters for return; band rules mainly save turnover ([Vanguard](https://corporate.vanguard.com/content/dam/corp/research/pdf/the_rebalancing_edge_optimizing_target_date_fund_rebalancing_through_threshold_based_strategies.pdf)).

**Gold is a probabilistic diversifier, not a guaranteed hedge.** From September 2024 to March 2025 it rose **+13.7%** while Nifty fell 15.1%, and a 60/30/10 equity/debt/gold mix held that episode's drawdown to −7.5% against −15.2% for Nifty. In Q1 2026 gold itself fell **26%** (29 January to 23 March) during an 11% Nifty fall, and an 80/20 equity/gold mix drew down no less than Nifty did. Gold's 34.6% CAGR in the short window inflates every gold-mix return, so those returns must not be extrapolated. The World Gold Council, an industry body, finds 7.5–15% gold improves risk-adjusted returns for a 70/30 INR portfolio over 19 years ([WGC 2026](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/research/why-gold-2026-anchor-indian-portfolios)). Gold and Nifty fell together in 2013 ([SafeGold](https://www.safegold.com/blog/gold-price-market/gold-price-during-market-crash-india/)). Silver behaves like leveraged gold (47% volatility, −45% drawdown, 0.83 daily correlation with gold) and is not a loss-control sleeve.

**International equity and debt are partly constrained.** Fresh subscriptions to international funds paused again in April–May 2026 under the $7 bn industry cap ([VMint Capital](https://vmintcapital.com/international-mutual-funds-india-investment-status-2026/)). Capped ETFs such as MON100 have traded at premiums of up to ~14% ([TradingQnA](https://tradingqna.com/t/kite-nudge-for-mon100-etf-inav/130815)), and silver ETFs reached 5.7% in October 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/mutual-fund/axis-tata-mf-suspend-investments-silver-etf-fof-supply-shortage-125101301245_1.html)). Debt funds bought after 1 April 2023 are taxed at slab rate, which makes arbitrage funds the tax-efficient short-term parking place for high-slab investors ([TaxClue](https://taxclue.in/blog/debt-mutual-fund-tax-post-2023-ita-2025)). Any allocation model must check the ETF premium to iNAV before suggesting a buy.

**Failure modes:** long bull markets (diversified mixes lag, per a FundsIndia study covering 2000–2026 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/can-adding-gold-and-debt-to-equities-boost-long-term-returns-while-reducing-portfolio-drawdowns-561126-2026-10-13))); a debt sell-off that coincides with an equity fall, as rising rates did in 2013; and gold crashing with equity. **Parameter sensitivity** is low. The 5pp band and the annual rebalance gave nearly identical results, which is the signature of a robust rule.

## Trend, volatility and drawdown rules mostly add whipsaw

**Trend filters.** *Hypothesis:* exit equity when the index is below its 10-month average and you dodge the middle of slow bear markets. *Mechanism:* the big bear markets of 2000–02 and 2008 unfolded over many months. Faber's 10-month rule cut a five-asset portfolio's drawdown from about 46% to under 10% ([QuantConnect summary](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/8285/a-quantitative-approach-to-tactical-asset-allocation-gtaa-5-with-simplemovingaverage/)). Hurst, Ooi and Pedersen find trend following positive in every decade since 1880 and positive in 8 of the 10 worst 60/40 drawdowns ([Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/portfolio-efficiency/)), but that is a leveraged long-short futures strategy, not long-only index timing. On the Nifty, trend algorithms worked in sharp falls and failed in gradual or trendless markets ([Indian Journal of Finance](https://www.indianjournalofentrepreneurship.com/index.php/IJF/article/view/71980)).

The repo's test reproduces both the promise and the fragility. The monthly 10-month filter cut max drawdown to **−14%**, but it made **13 switches** in 9.4 years. **Five of its seven exits cost 0.2–4.5%** against staying invested, and four of those seven exits would have realised short-term gains taxed at **20% STCG** ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/finance/personal-finance/budget-2024-all-the-key-changes-to-capital-gains-tax-explained-124072500330_1.html)), which the test did not model. Its COVID result (+18.6% in 2020 against +13.8%) was **luck of timing**: the month-end signal happened to fire on 2 March 2020, before the worst days. The "safer" 2-month-confirm variant exited on 1 April 2020, after the bottom, missed a **+36.7% rebound**, and was the worst rule tested. In the September 2024 to March 2025 grind lower the filter exited only on 1 January 2025, after most of the fall, and the benefit had fully reversed by June 2025. When the strategy is re-run on resampled paths, its median CAGR cost is **−1.7pp** with only a 74% chance of a shallower drawdown. *Parameter sensitivity is high:* one extra month of confirmation turned a helpful rule into a harmful one.

**Volatility targeting** cuts left tails because crashes tend to arrive when volatility is already high ([Harvey et al., Duke](https://people.duke.edu/~charvey/Research/Published_Papers/P135_The_impact_of.pdf)). However, the claimed alpha fails in real-time tests: out-of-sample versions underperform the unmanaged portfolio in 72 of 103 strategies, gross of costs ([Cederburg et al.](https://www.lehigh.edu/~xuy219/research/COWY.pdf)). In the repo test a 15% target barely engaged. Nifty was calm going into February 2020, and the rule then de-risked late into the recovery (2020 return −3.4%).

**Drawdown triggers and CPPI** bound losses only in continuous-trading theory. In practice they suffer gap risk and cash-lock after crashes ([Quantpedia](https://quantpedia.com/introduction-to-cppi-constant-proportion-portfolio-insurance/)). The repo's −15% rule cut equity on **4 March 2025, the exact Nifty trough**, stayed at 50% until November, and ended with a worse 2024–26 drawdown (−16.9%) than buy-and-hold. In the realised-path bootstrap its CAGR cost was the only statistically significant result, at P(gain) = 0.04.

**The caveat that governs all of this:** the repo's windows contain **no deep, slow bear market**. COVID was a V-shaped crash, and the rest were 15% corrections. That is the worst environment for trend and drawdown rules and the best for buy-and-hold, so the tests cannot show these rules' main benefit. Equally, nothing in them validates the rules. A proper verdict needs 2005–2026 data that includes 2008.

## Options and shorting are real tools, but SEBI's lot size prices small investors out

**The genuine options edge is selling insurance, not buying it.** Option buyers pay a volatility risk premium. Over 1986–2011 Cboe's put-write index grew 1,153% and its buy-write index 830%, against 807% for the S&P 500 and only 368% for the collar index ([Mondo Visione](https://mondovisione.com/media-and-resources/news/new-study-compares-25-year-performance-of-options-strategy-benchmarks-to-traditi-2012214/)). The collar index earned 3.2% a year in excess return against 7.3% for the index ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/equity-options/equity-index-collar-performance)). Israelov's "Pathetic Protection" finds that protective puts reduce drawdowns less than simply holding less equity at equal cost. Over 250-day windows, the protected portfolio's max drawdown was −32.1% against −20.9% for the equally costly divested one ([AQR](https://www.aqr.com/Insights/Research/White-Papers/Pathetic-Protection-The-Elusive-Benefits-of-Protective-Puts)). Put-writing and covered calls earn the premium by being short crashes and by capping rallies. Their "income" is crash insurance sold to someone else. Indian long-run evidence is thin; no NSE buy-write or put-write index factsheet was found.

**Capital is the binding constraint.** Since October 2024 SEBI requires index derivative contracts to be worth **₹15–20 lakh** at review ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/sebi-announces-six-key-changes-to-curb-speculation-in-derivatives-trading-124100101316_1.html)). The Nifty lot became **65 units from 30 December 2025** ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/marketintel/bulletin/429705/revision-in-lot-size-of-index-derivative-contracts-from-december-30-2025)), about ₹16–17 lakh notional. Hedge ratios come only in whole lots, so a portfolio smaller than one lot gets either 0% or an over-hedge that turns into a bearish bet. A covered call requires owning a full lot of the underlying. By the researcher's Black-Scholes estimate (unverified, no skew), a 3-month 5% out-of-the-money Nifty put costs about 0.4–1.5% of notional depending on implied volatility, roughly 1.6–6% a year if rolled quarterly. On a ₹6.75 lakh book with one ₹16.6 lakh lot that is about **4–7% of portfolio value a year**, most of the long-run equity premium. Moving 20% to debt cost 0.7pp in the repo test.

**Behaviour and tax raise the bar further.** SEBI found **about 91% of individual equity-derivative traders lost money in FY25**, with net losses of ₹1,05,603 crore ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/capital-market-news/sebi-sees-dip-in-derivatives-turnover-91-of-retail-traders-lose-money-in-fy25-125070800660_1.html)). FY26 losses were ₹91,685 crore at an average of ₹1.17 lakh per trader ([Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/retail-fo-traders-lose-rs91685-crore-in-fy26-despite-sebi-curbs-average-loss-hits-rs117-lakh-govt/81318.html)). That is mostly speculation, not hedging, but drift from one to the other is common. F&O profit is **non-speculative business income taxed at slab rate**, and losses carried forward offset only business income ([TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1068347)). A hedge that loses in bull years and wins in crashes is therefore taxed asymmetrically against 12.5% LTCG on the equity. As reported, STT rose from 1 April 2026 to **0.05% on futures and 0.15% on option premium sold** ([Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/stt-hike-from-april-1-2026-budget-what-it-means-for-futures-and-options-traders)). This is from press summaries; verify it against the Finance Act. SEBI's retail algorithmic-trading framework was **not researched** in these notes. Anyone automating order placement through a broker API must check its registration and broker-approval rules first.

**Short selling** in cash is intraday-only for retail. Overnight shorts need futures (the same large lots) or securities lending and borrowing, which runs on exchange platforms but is thin and operationally clunky ([Zerodha Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/trending/shorting-and-indian-capital-markets); [Angel One](https://oga-prod.angelone.in/knowledge-center/share-market/securities-lending-and-borrowing)). Indian stock-level time-series momentum exists but suffers severe crashes ([RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/jefasp/jefas-08-2021-0159.html)). Selling part of the long book gives the same exposure cut with no margin, no squeeze risk and understood tax. **Futures trend following** has the best crisis record of any return-seeking strategy. But a diversified version needs several ₹15 lakh-plus contracts across equity, currency and commodity markets, with daily mark-to-market. For an individual below about ₹50 lakh in risk capital (an inference, not sourced) it is out of reach.

**Data for an honest test exists but is coarse.** NSE F&O bhavcopy gives end-of-day option settle prices by strike and expiry, and the R package `nser` fetches it from 2016 (old format) and 2020 (new UDiFF format) ([nser vignette](https://cran.nics.utk.edu/cran/web/packages/nser/vignettes/fobhav.html)). It has no bid-ask spreads, so a backtest must add explicit spread haircuts, model integer lots and the lot-size history (Nov 2024, Dec 2025), and never assume fractional contracts.

## What changes at ₹6.75 lakh

At the current portfolio size the ranking collapses to its top rows. An index option lot is about 2.5 times the whole portfolio, so puts, collars, covered calls, put-writing, futures shorts and futures trend following are all infeasible as risk tools. Any position would be either zero or a leveraged bet. Stock-lot covered calls need ₹5 lakh-plus per stock against an average holding of about ₹20k (stock lot minimum unverified). The levers that work at this size are all divisible: the equity/debt/gold split, position and concentration caps, and directing new money to underweight sleeves. The portfolio has no debt or international sleeve today and about 6% in one gold ETF. Choosing a drawdown budget is the single most consequential loss-control decision available. As a rough guide, Nifty fell 36% in 2020 and about 43% in 2008 ([BloombergQuint](https://www.bloombergquint.com/amp/charts/nifty-and-rupee-no-longer-correlated)), so a 70% equity weight implies roughly a 25–30% portfolio drawdown in a 2008-type year, before any diversification benefit. Rebalance with new cash first, harvest equity gains within the ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption, and avoid selling lots held under 12 months.

## Conclusion

The research reframes the question. "Can a model avoid losses?" has a trivial yes (hold debt) and a practical no (not without giving up most of the equity return). The useful question is how much return you are willing to pay per point of drawdown, and in which bear markets you want to be paid. Static allocation pays a small, certain premium for protection in every kind of fall. Trend rules pay a larger, uncertain premium that only slow bear markets repay, and the repo has not yet seen one. Options buyers pay the highest premium of all, and only options sellers earn it, by accepting crash risk. Below roughly ₹20–50 lakh, Indian market structure settles the matter: the divisible tools are also the ones with the best evidence.

**Decisions for you:** (1) Your drawdown budget, i.e. the peak-to-trough loss you would sit through without selling, which sets the equity weight. (2) Target sleeves, for example around 60–70% equity, 20–30% debt or arbitrage, 10% gold, with international only when funds are open and near NAV. (3) Whether a trend-status signal should be shown at all, given that it whipsawed in five of seven exits here. (4) Whether to fund the data build (debt series, long gold, Nifty TRI from 2005) before any further rule testing. (5) Whether F&O should stay out of scope until capital and a preregistered, paper-traded test justify it. Verify regulatory and tax figures (STT rates, lot sizes, Income-tax Act 2025 renumbering) against primary SEBI, NSE and Finance Act texts before acting.
