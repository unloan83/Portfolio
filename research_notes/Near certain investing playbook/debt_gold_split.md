# Debt, Gold and the Equity/Debt/Gold Split — Indian retail investor, Oct 2026

Research date: 16 Oct 2026. Category-level guidance, not personalised advice. Several AMC/tax primary sites (ICICI Pru AMC, ABSL AMC, Kotak Neo, Business Today, Calm Substack) could not be fetched from this environment (DNS failure). Repo context (from the caller, not re-verified here): 20% debt cut Nifty max drawdown from about −36% to about −30%, at a cost of about 0.7 pp/yr. Gold helped in Sep 2024–Mar 2025 but fell 26% in Q1 2026.

## 1. Debt options: roles, liquidity, risk, current yields

### Takeaway
For most people the "debt sleeve" is boring on purpose. Use liquid or money-market funds (or an FD) for money needed in 0–1 year. Use arbitrage funds as a tax-efficient substitute if you are in a high tax bracket and can hold for more than a year. Use PPF, EPF/VPF and the RBI Floating Rate Bond for long-term fixed income that is effectively risk-free. Long gilt and constant-maturity gilt funds are interest-rate bets, not a safe cushion. Rates turned upward in Oct 2026: RBI hiked the repo rate to 5.50% and set a "calibrated tightening" stance. That rate rise hurts long-duration funds and helps short-term yields.

### Inferences
Category roles below are from general product knowledge and SEBI category definitions, not separately sourced here.
- **Savings account / sweep FD:** for the first 1–2 months of expenses. Instant access. Interest is taxed at slab.
- **Bank FD:** for known, dated needs and as part of the emergency fund. Principal is DICGC-insured up to ₹5 lakh per bank. There is a premature-withdrawal penalty. Interest is taxed at slab every year on accrual, with TDS.
- **Overnight fund:** near-zero risk, yields about SDF/repo (about 5.25–5.5% now). It is a parking place, not an investment.
- **Liquid / money-market fund:** the default debt-sleeve and emergency-fund vehicle. T+1 redemption, small rate and credit risk, and gains taxed at slab only when you redeem, so you can defer tax. Expected gross yield is probably about 6–6.5% after the hike, roughly 3-month CP (6.4%) less costs. **This is an estimate; check factsheets.**
- **Arbitrage fund:** debt-like risk (hedged equity) with equity taxation. Best for people in the 20–30% slab with a holding period of more than 12 months. In calm markets returns can trail liquid funds.
- **Short-duration / corporate bond / banking-PSU funds:** moderate duration (about 1–4 yrs). They suit a 2–4 year horizon. Small credit risk. Taxed at slab, so their only edge over an FD is tax deferral.
- **Gilt / 10Y constant-maturity gilt:** no credit risk but large interest-rate risk. With a modified duration of about 7, a 1 pp rise in yields means about a −7% price fall. In a hiking cycle (Oct 2026) they can lose money. Treat them as an optional tactical holding, not as the core of the sleeve.
- **Target-maturity index funds (Bharat Bond, SDL/G-sec TMFs):** the return is roughly the entry YTM if held to maturity. Good for matching a dated goal. NAV swings before maturity. Taxed at slab.
- **PPF:** 7.1% tax-free (EEE), 15-yr lock-in with partial withdrawals from year 7. Max ₹1.5 lakh/yr.
- **EPF/VPF:** 8.25%. Effectively locked until job change, retirement or specific withdrawals.
- **NPS (scheme C/G):** debt inside NPS, locked until 60. Counts as long-term debt.
- **RBI FRSB:** 8.05% now, 7-yr lock (earlier exit only for seniors). Interest is taxable at slab and is not cumulative (paid half-yearly).
- **What counts as "the debt sleeve":** count everything that is fixed income: EPF, PPF, NPS-debt, FDs, FRSB and debt or arbitrage funds. Exclude the emergency fund, and exclude money earmarked for goals within 1–2 years. Locked products (EPF/PPF) are "long-term debt" that cannot be used to rebalance quickly. Keep some liquid/arbitrage money as the "rebalancing debt".

## 2. Tax for FY2026-27 (Income-tax Act 2025 in force from 1 Apr 2026)

### Takeaway
The rates carry over from the 2024 framework. Debt funds bought after 1 Apr 2023 are taxed at slab regardless of holding period. Arbitrage funds are taxed as equity (20% under 12 months; 12.5% above 12 months beyond the ₹1.25 lakh exemption). Gold ETFs pay 12.5% LTCG after 12 months, gold FoFs after 24 months. FD interest is taxed at slab with TDS. PPF is EEE. The big 2026 change: **the SGB maturity exemption now applies only to original subscribers who hold to maturity. Secondary-market SGB buyers pay capital-gains tax from tax year 2026-27.**

### Cited Findings
- Debt ("specified") mutual funds bought on or after 1 Apr 2023 are taxed at slab rate for any holding period. Under the Act 2025 a "specified mutual fund" is one with more than 65% in debt and money-market instruments. Units bought before 1 Apr 2023 pay 12.5% (no indexation) after 24 months. — [ICICI Pru Tax Reckoner 2026-27](https://www.icicipruamc.com/blob/knowledgecentre/tax_reckoner/Tax%20Reckoner%202026-27.pdf) (snippet; PDF not fetchable); [IncorpX](https://www.incorpx.io/blog/income-tax-mutual-fund-returns-2026); [Kuvera](https://kuvera.in/blog/?p=42409)
- Equity-oriented funds, including arbitrage: 20% STCG (≤12 months) and 12.5% LTCG above ₹1.25 lakh/yr. The ₹1.25 lakh limit is shared with all equity holdings. — [HDFC Bank](https://www.hdfc.bank.in/blogs/mutual-funds/mutual-fund-taxation-in-fy-2026-27); [Jainam](https://www.jainam.in/blog/arbitrage-fund-vs-liquid-fund/)
- Gold ETFs: 12.5% LTCG after 12 months, slab rate below that. Gold FoFs: LTCG after 24 months (unlisted). — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/?p=1057273); [OnePercentClub](https://www.onepercentclub.io/blog/?p=1643); search summary noted that sources disagree on 12 vs 24 months for gold FoFs.
- SGB: Finance Bill 2026 (Clause 35, amending s.70(1)(x) of the Income-tax Act 2025) limits the redemption exemption to bonds "subscribed to by a subscriber at the time of original issue and held continuously until redemption on maturity". It applies from 1 Apr 2026, to tax year 2026-27 onward. Secondary-market buyers pay 12.5% LTCG if held more than 12 months, or slab rate if held less. The 2.5% interest is still taxed at slab, with no TDS. — [TaxTMI notes (clause text)](https://www.taxtmi.com/tmi_notes?id=2402); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/opinion-budget-curtails-tax-free-status-of-sovereign-gold-bonds); [Moneylife](https://moneylife.in/article/bought-sovereign-gold-bonds-from-the-market-budget-2026-brings-a-capital-gains-tax-shock/79547.html); [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/investing-guide/articles/budget-2026-sgb-tax-changes/). Whether the change is "retrospective" for existing secondary holders is disputed: [Vision IAS / Business Standard](https://visionias.in/current-affairs/upsc-daily-news-summary/article/2026-02-16/business-standard/economy/sovereign-gold-bond-issue-why-tax-certainty-matters-for-investors) says yes; Moneylife says original subscribers are unaffected.
- SGB issuance: no new tranche since Feb 2024 and no FY2026-27 issuance calendar. Existing bonds trade on NSE/BSE. No official "discontinued" notice was found. — [GoldenPi](https://goldenpi.com/blog/bond-news/sovereign-gold-bond-scheme-discontinued-for-new-issues/); [RetireWise](https://www.retirewise.in/?p=11280)
- EPF/VPF: interest on employee contributions above ₹2.5 lakh/yr is taxable, with TDS. — [CalcGuru](https://calcguru.in/?p=4295)

### Inferences
- From general knowledge, not re-sourced here: FD interest is taxed at slab on accrual, with bank TDS at 10% above the threshold. PPF is EEE. FRSB interest is taxed at slab. Target-maturity debt index funds and Bharat Bond are "specified" debt funds, so they are taxed at slab (an older source's 20%-with-indexation rule no longer applies).
- In practice: at the 30% slab, arbitrage funds beat liquid funds after tax for money held more than 1 year. At the 0–10% slab, liquid funds or FDs are fine. For gold, an ETF held more than 12 months gets the same 12.5% treatment that a secondary SGB now gets. A secondary SGB keeps the 2.5% coupon (taxable) and has no expense ratio, but trades at a premium or discount and is often illiquid.

## 3. Gold options and silver

### Takeaway
Use a low-cost gold ETF (expense ratio about 0.5% or lower, low tracking error) if you have a demat account. Use a gold FoF only if you need a SIP without demat, accepting the extra cost layer and the 24-month LTCG threshold. A secondary-market SGB lost its tax-free-maturity edge in 2026, so buy one only at a discount. Avoid digital gold (unregulated) and jewellery as investments. Gold is a crisis and currency hedge with deep drawdowns of its own: −26% in Q1 2026 per the repo tests. Silver is more volatile and partly industrial, so it does not serve as a defensive asset.

### Cited Findings
- Gold hit a record near $5,589/oz in Jan 2026 and then corrected sharply from end-January. — [WGC India update Feb/Mar 2026](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/03/india-gold-market-update-volatility-softens-demand); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/gold-etf-inflows-india-june-2026-wgc-report)

H1 2026 cumulative inflows were $3.90 bn (WGC). — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/gold-etf-inflows-india-june-2026-wgc-report); [Sahi](https://www.sahi.com/blogs/india-gold-etf-inflows-crash-78-percent-february-2026); [WGC Apr 2026](https://www.gold.org/goldhub/gold-focus/2026/04/india-gold-market-update-mixed-reading)

### Inferences
- **Gold ETF:** cheapest liquid route. Needs demat plus brokerage. Check its liquidity (bid-ask spread) on the exchange.
- **Gold FoF:** SIP-friendly with no demat, but costs more and needs 24 months for LTCG.
- **SGB (secondary):** 2.5% coupon and no expense ratio. Since 2026 the maturity gain is taxable for secondary buyers. Thin trading, so buy only below the gold-implied value. Early exit through RBI is possible after year 5 on interest dates.
- **Digital gold:** not SEBI or RBI regulated. Typically 3% GST plus a buy-sell spread of a few percent.
- **Physical gold / jewellery:** making charges, 3% GST and purity risk. Not an investment vehicle.
- **Silver:** a large industrial-demand share and historically higher volatility than gold. It tends to fall with risk assets in growth scares. Not a defensive diversifier; at most a small satellite. Silver volatility is general knowledge and was not sourced here.
- Size gold at about 5–15% and rebalance it. The Sep 2024–Mar 2025 help and the −26% Q1 2026 fall both show that gold is a separate risky asset that lowers combined drawdowns only because its crashes rarely line up with equity crashes.

## 4. Choosing the split; where EPF/PPF/FDs fit

### Takeaway
Build the emergency fund first (about 6 months of expenses in a savings account, FD or liquid fund). Count it outside the portfolio. Then choose equity % with the rule **equity % ≈ (worst portfolio fall you can live with) ÷ 50%**, since Indian equity has fallen about 50–60% peak to trough (−59.5% max drawdown over 20 years in the FundsIndia data). Put about 10% in gold and the rest in debt. Count EPF, PPF, NPS-debt and FDs as part of the debt sleeve. Rebalance once a year or when a sleeve drifts by about 5 pp or more.

### Cited Findings
- FundsIndia data via Business Today, 13 Oct 2026, 20 years: 100% equity returned 11% CAGR with a −59.5% max drawdown. A 70/15/15 equity/debt/gold mix returned 12.3% CAGR with a −29.2% max drawdown. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/story/can-adding-gold-and-debt-to-equities-boost-long-term-returns-while-reducing-portfolio-drawdowns-561126-2026-10-13) (snippet; full page not fetchable; worst calendar year not given)
- A Calm Substack backtest from Jul 1990 uses Nifty TRI, a 1-yr SBI FD and gold. Allocations that always held some debt had lower drawdowns than Nifty alone. — [Calm Substack](https://calm.substack.com/p/how-to-think-about-asset-allocation) (snippet only)
- An Axis Multi-Asset FoF deck shows 2011–2025 rolling returns for 45/45/10 and 65/20/15 mixes. This is marketing material. — [Axis MF deck](https://www.axismf.com///cms/sites/default/files/pdf-factsheets/Axis%20Multi%20Asset%20Active%20FoF%20-%20NFO%20Deck.pdf)
- Freefincal's own allocation was about 47/19/8 (plus REITs), with debt MFs used for emergencies, buying equity in down months (Mar 2026) and rebalancing. — [freefincal](https://freefincal.com/?p=350905)

### Inferences
- **Arithmetic rule:** portfolio fall ≈ equity% × equity fall + gold% × gold fall + debt% × about 0. With equity −50%, gold flat and debt +3%, a 60/30/10 portfolio falls about −29%, 70/20/10 about −34%, and 40/50/10 about −18%. These are illustrative, not historical.
- Consistency check: FundsIndia's 70/15/15 at −29.2% max drawdown is milder than this arithmetic suggests. Gold rose during 2008 and 2020, and rebalancing helped. The repo's 80/20 at about −30% (Nifty −36%) is consistent with the rule.
- **Age/horizon:** money needed within 3 years goes in debt only. For 3–7 years, keep equity at 40% or less. For 7+ years, use the tolerance rule. "100 − age" is a crude upper bound for equity.
- **Where existing assets fit:** EPF, PPF, NPS-debt, FDs and FRSB are all debt. A salaried 30-year-old with a large EPF may already be "40% debt" without owning any debt fund. Count EPF at current balance and keep new SIPs mostly in equity until the target is reached. The emergency fund and near-term goal money stay outside the split. Real estate and the home are excluded.
- **Rebalancing sleeve:** because EPF/PPF can't be sold, hold at least a few % of the portfolio in liquid or arbitrage funds so you can actually buy equity after a crash.

## Debt and gold decision tree

1. **Do you have 6 months of expenses set aside?** If no, build it first in a savings account, sweep FD and liquid fund. Stop here until it's done.
2. **Is the money needed within 1 year?** Use a liquid or money-market fund, or an FD. Not part of the portfolio split.
3. **Is it needed in 1–3 years, or on a fixed date?** Use an FD ladder, a target-maturity fund matched to the date, or a short-duration / banking-PSU fund.
4. **Long-term debt sleeve (3+ years):**
   - Using the PPF limit (₹1.5 lakh/yr) and EPF/VPF first makes sense for most people: tax-free or high, government-backed, illiquid.
   - Want 8%-ish, taxable, with a 7-yr lock? Use the RBI FRSB (8.05% Jul–Dec 2026).
   - For the part you need to keep liquid for rebalancing: at the 20–30% slab with a 12+ month hold, use an arbitrage fund. At a 0–10% slab, use a liquid or money-market fund.
   - Gilt or constant-maturity gilt only if you understand that a 1 pp rise in yields is roughly a −7% price fall. Avoid as core in the current hiking cycle.
   - Avoid credit-risk funds, and avoid chasing yield.
5. **Gold sleeve (about 5–15%):**
   - With a demat account, buy a low-cost gold ETF (expense ratio of about 0.5% or lower, high volume).
   - Without demat, or for a SIP, use a gold FoF (higher cost, 24-month LTCG threshold).
   - Secondary SGB only at a discount to gold value. The maturity gain is taxable for you since Apr 2026.
   - Skip digital gold, jewellery and silver as "defensive" holdings.
6. **Rebalance yearly, or when a sleeve drifts by 5 pp or more.** Do it with new money or with the liquid/arbitrage sleeve. Use tax-free or low-tax moves first.

## Split-selection rule of thumb table

Assumes equity −50%, debt about 0 to +3%, gold flat to +10% in a crash. These are illustrative arithmetic, not guarantees, and real crashes can be worse (Indian equity's 20-yr max drawdown was −59.5%).

| Worst portfolio fall you can sit through | Equity | Debt (incl. EPF/PPF/FD) | Gold | Approx. crash-case fall | Typical fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| about −10% | 20% | 70% | 10% | −8% to −10% | Retiree / money needed in 3–5 yrs |
| about −15% | 30% | 60% | 10% | −13% to −15% | Conservative |
| about −20% | 40% | 50% | 10% | −18% to −20% | Moderate-conservative |
| about −25% | 50% | 40% | 10% | −23% to −25% | Balanced |
| about −30% | 60% | 30% | 10% | −28% to −30% | Moderate growth (60/30/10) |
| about −35% | 70% | 20% | 10% | −33% to −35% | Growth (70/20/10). FundsIndia's 70/15/15 had a −29.2% max DD over 20 yrs |
| about −40%+ | 80% | 10–15% | 5–10% | −38% to −40% | Aggressive, 10+ yr horizon. The repo's 80/20 gave about −30% vs Nifty −36% |

Rules: (1) Emergency fund and money needed within 3 years sit outside the table. (2) Count EPF, PPF, NPS-debt and FDs in "Debt". (3) Keep gold at 10% or less unless you deliberately want more. Gold alone fell 26% in Q1 2026. (4) Pick the row once and change it only when your life situation changes, not after a market move.
