## 1. What is an index fund vs an ETF in India, how do you buy each, and when should you pick which?

### Takeaway
Both hold the same index basket. An **index fund** is bought from the fund house (AMC) at the day's closing NAV, from a bank account, with easy SIPs from about ₹100. An **ETF** trades on NSE/BSE like a share through a demat account at a live market price, which can drift from its fair value (iNAV). ETFs usually have lower TERs (around 0.03–0.05% for big Nifty 50 ETFs, vs 0.06–0.20% for the cheapest Nifty 50 index funds). They add brokerage/DP costs and a premium/discount risk, so they suit demat holders putting in lump sums or larger chunks. Index funds suit hands-off monthly SIPs.

### Cited Findings
- **ETF mechanics:** NIFTYBEES is listed and traded on NSE. It can be bought and sold through a broker throughout the trading day, and limit orders are possible. NSE says its liquidity comes from investor trading, arbitrage with index futures, and authorised participants arbitraging against the underlying shares. — [NSE Nifty BeES page](https://nseindia.com/products/content/equities/indices/prod_niftyBees.htm)
- **iNAV rule:** iNAV (indicative NAV, the live "fair value" of one ETF unit) must be published within a 15-second lag of the underlying market for equity ETFs, under SEBI's passive-fund framework (circular dated 23 May 2022). — [SCC/SEBI summary via search](https://scconline.com/blog/post/2021/07/31/sebi-issues-circular-on-intra-day-net-asset-value-nav-for-transactions-in-units-of-exchange-traded-funds-directly-with-asset-management-companies/amp); [WIRC-ICAI SEBI Aug 2022 update](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/law-updates/sebi-aug-2022.pdf)
- **Market makers:** each AMC must sign up at least two market makers per ETF, who must give two-way quotes (buy and sell prices) during minimum trading periods. Zerodha Fund House's policy cites the SEBI Master Circular of 27 Jun 2024. — [Zerodha Fund House market-making policy](https://assets.zerodhafundhouse.com/statutory-reports/other-disclosures/Policy%20on%20Market%20Making%20in%20ETFs.pdf)
- **ETF price bands:** ETFs have traded with a fixed ±20% price band. SEBI has considered using the previous day's iNAV as the base price for the band. One source mentions a 15 Jun 2026 circular on this, but I could not confirm that it is final or when it takes effect. — [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/invest/mutual-funds/sebi-to-consider-inav-based-price-bands-for-etfs-know-how-investors-will-be-impacted)
- **STT on ETFs:** one secondary source says there is no STT (securities transaction tax) when buying an equity ETF, and 0.001% STT on the sell value of a delivery sale. INDmoney's STT table gives 0.001% for sales of equity-oriented fund units. Upstox's own charge table shows 0.1% delivery STT on both sides for stocks, with no separate ETF line. **Unverified against the Finance Act. Treat as likely but confirm.** — [INDmoney STT](https://www.indmoney.com/articles/stocks/meaning-of-stt-in-share-market); [Upstox charges](https://upstox.com/pricing/)
- **DP charge on ETF sales (Upstox):** ₹20 + GST per scrip per day on sells from demat holdings (equity delivery), of which ₹3.5 is the CDSL portion. I found nothing saying ETFs are exempt. — [Upstox help: pricing and charges](https://upstox.com/help-center/257381)
- **Index fund minimums:** Navi Nifty 50 Index Fund Direct has a minimum SIP of ₹100 and no exit load (Kuvera listing). UTI Nifty 50 Index Fund has a minimum initial investment of ₹1,000 and nil exit load. — [Kuvera Navi Nifty 50](https://kuvera.in/mutual-funds/fund/navi-nifty-50-index-fund-direct-plan-growth--ESNIGD-GR); [TaxGuru summary of UTI scheme info](https://taxguru.in/finance/mutual-funds/uti-nifty-50-index-fund-features-ter-taxation-investor-risks.html)
- **ETF TERs are lower than index fund TERs:** NIFTYBEES 0.04%, SETFNIF50 about 0.04–0.05%, ICICI NIFTYIETF 0.03%, HDFCNIFTY 0.05%, UTI NIFTYBETA 0.05% (sharpely comparison pages, about Mar 2026). — [sharpely NIFTYBEES vs SBI](https://sharpely.in/etfs/compare/nippon-india-nifty-bees-vs-sbi-nifty-50-etf); [sharpely ICICI vs NIFTYBEES](https://sharpely.in/etfs/compare/icici-prudential-nifty-50-etf-vs-nippon-india-nifty-bees); [sharpely HDFC vs NIFTYBEES](https://sharpely.in/etfs/compare/hdfc-nifty-50-etf-vs-nippon-india-nifty-bees)
- **Nifty ETF liquidity:** Nifty ETFs are much more liquid than Sensex ETFs because traders, fund managers and institutions use them for arbitrage and hedging. — [Advisorkhoj](https://www.advisorkhoj.com/articles/mutual-funds/nippon-india-etf-nifty-bees:-a-good-long-term-investment-in-current-market)
- **Overall ETF trading:** NSE ETF traded value was ₹1.19 trillion in FY24 through December, about twice pre-pandemic levels. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/etf-trading-turnover-tops-rs-1-trillion-for-second-year-in-a-row-124022000866_1.html)

### Inferences
- **Plain-language comparison:**

  | | Index fund (direct plan) | ETF |
  |---|---|---|
  | Where you buy | AMC website, MF Central, Kuvera, Coin, Groww, Upstox MF section | Stock exchange via broker and demat (e.g., Upstox) |
  | Price you get | That day's closing NAV, after cut-off | Live market price, which can be above or below iNAV |
  | SIP | Native, auto-debit from bank, from ₹100–₹500 | Only via a broker "stock SIP" or manual monthly buys |
  | Typical TER (Nifty 50) | ~0.06–0.20% | ~0.03–0.05% |
  | Other costs | None (no brokerage, no DP; small stamp duty) | Brokerage (often ₹0 for delivery at discount brokers, check), DP charge on sell, bid-ask spread, possible premium |
  | Fractional amounts | Yes (₹ amount buys fractional units) | Whole units only |
  | Risk of overpaying | None | Yes, if you buy at a premium to iNAV or in a thin ETF |

- **Rule of thumb:** for monthly SIPs below about ₹10–25k with no wish to watch prices, the 0.05–0.15% TER gap in favour of ETFs is worth roughly ₹50–150 a year per ₹1 lakh. That is easily wiped out by one bad fill or a premium, so an index fund is simpler. For larger lump sums placed in a demat account, a highly liquid ETF bought with a limit order saves a little each year.
- Avoid small or illiquid ETFs. Their market price can sit at a noticeable premium or discount to iNAV even with market makers.

### Gaps
- I found no current per-ETF daily traded value (turnover) figures for Oct 2026. Check NSE's "ETF" market-watch page (nseindia.com → Market Data → ETF) for live volume and value.
- I found no data on typical bid-ask spreads or premium/discount levels for NIFTYBEES and peers in 2026.
- Upstox's current delivery brokerage for ETFs, and whether a broker "ETF SIP" feature exists on Upstox, are not verified.

## 2. Direct vs regular plan: what it means, how to spot one, where to buy, and how to switch (with tax)

### Takeaway
Every mutual fund scheme has two versions with the same portfolio. The **Regular** plan pays a yearly commission to a distributor out of the fund, so it has a higher TER. The **Direct** plan pays no commission. You can tell them apart by the word "Direct" in the scheme name and the lower TER. Buy direct through the AMC's own site, MF Central, or a direct-plan platform (Zerodha Coin and Kuvera are direct-only; Groww and Upstox offer direct plans at no commission). Switching regular to direct is treated as a sale plus a purchase, so it can trigger capital gains tax and possibly an exit load.

### Cited Findings
- **Fee gap example:** Navi Nifty 50 Index Fund launched with a 0.06% direct TER vs 0.26% for the regular plan, which is bought through an intermediary. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/article/pf/index-funds-under-focus-as-navi-mf-looks-to-disrupt-the-market-121062901402_1.html); [Money9](https://www.money9.com/news/exclusive/indias-cheapest-fund-navi-nifty-50-index-fund-raises-rs-100-crore-through-nfo-57923.html)
- **Another gap:** UTI Nifty Next 50 Index Fund direct TER 0.42% vs regular 0.87% (fund facts 31 Aug 2026, secondary summary). — [TaxGuru](https://taxguru.in/finance/mutual-funds/uti-nifty-50-index-fund-features-ter-taxation-investor-risks.html)
- **Zerodha Coin:** Zerodha says Coin offers only direct funds and has no plans to offer regular plans or to charge investors. — [Zerodha Z-Connect](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/?p=452330)
- **Kuvera:** described as direct-plan-only. It earns no distributor commissions, although it holds AMFI distributor registration, which even direct-only platforms need. — [Equities India Kuvera review](https://equitiesindia.com/tools-directory/kuvera-review)
- **Groww:** started in 2016 as a direct mutual fund platform. It is commission-free for direct funds but also sells stocks and other products. — [Free Press Journal 2026](https://www.freepressjournal.in/focused-view/best-platforms-to-invest-in-mutual-funds-in-india-2026)
- **Where else:** Zerodha Fund House lists MF Central, Coin, Groww, Kuvera and others as platforms where investors can buy direct "without any fees/charges". — [Zerodha Fund House help](https://help.zerodhafundhouse.com/support/solutions/articles/84000388848-how-can-i-invest-in-zerodha-fund-house-funds-)
- **Upstox:** describes its mutual funds as direct plans with no charges. — [Upstox invest](https://upstox.com/invest/)
- **Switching is taxable:** moving from regular to direct of the same scheme counts as a redemption plus a fresh purchase, so it is liable to capital gains tax. — [Stableinvestor](https://stableinvestor.com/2020/09/switch-regular-direct-taxation.html)
- **Current equity MF tax rates:** gains on units held 12 months or less are taxed at 20%. Gains on units held longer are taxed at 12.5%, after the first ₹1.25 lakh of long-term gains each financial year, which is exempt. One source says Budget 2026 left these unchanged. — [Tata MF blog](https://www.tatamutualfund.com/blogs/tax-efficiency-125-advantage); [Kotak Neo](https://www.kotakneo.com/news/market-news/ltcg-vs-stcg-capital-gains-tax-stocks-mutual-funds/)
- **Exit load on switching:** a 5paisa news report says SEBI removed exit loads for regular-to-direct switches within the same scheme. **Not confirmed against the SEBI circular.** Older sources say exit loads may apply. — [5paisa](https://5paisa.com/news/sebi-eliminates-exit-loads-for-switching-from-regular-to-direct-mutual-fund-plans); [Stableinvestor](https://stableinvestor.com/2020/09/switch-regular-direct-taxation.html)
- **Switch how-to and timing:** Upstox has a how-to guide. Spreading switches across financial years to use the ₹1.25 lakh exemption is a common tactic. — [Upstox guide](https://upstox.com/?p=24946); [Morningstar India](https://www.morningstar.in/posts/62912/switch-direct-plan.aspx)

### Inferences
- **How to check you are in a direct plan:** on the CAS (Consolidated Account Statement from CAMS/KFintech/MF Central), the scheme name says "Direct Plan – Growth". A regular plan shows an ARN code (the distributor's licence number) next to it. A direct plan shows "Direct" or no ARN.
- **Switching sensibly:** stop new SIPs into the regular plan now and start them in the direct plan. For existing regular units, switch the ones held more than 12 months (12.5% LTCG; use the ₹1.25 lakh yearly exemption first). Wait on units under 12 months (20% STCG) and units still inside any exit-load period. Index funds often have nil or short exit loads (e.g., 15 days at Motilal Oswal Nifty 500).
- Pick the **Growth** option rather than IDCW (payouts) for a long-term core. This avoids forced taxable payouts.

### Gaps
- Whether the "no exit load on regular-to-direct switch" rule is a binding SEBI circular, and from what date, is not confirmed.
- MF Utilities' current status was not checked.

## 3. Which index for the core: Nifty 50 vs Nifty 100 vs Nifty 500 vs Next 50 / Midcap 150

### Takeaway
Nifty 50 (the 50 largest companies) is the simplest, cheapest and most liquid core, but it is concentrated: HDFC Bank alone is about 10%, and financials dominate. Nifty 500 (about 92% of NSE free-float market cap) adds mid and small caps. Because it is market-cap weighted, it still behaves mostly like large caps, with slightly deeper historical drawdowns (about −64% vs −60%). Nifty Next 50 and Midcap 150 are satellites, not cores: they are more volatile, and their outperformance over Nifty 50 "comes and goes". **Simple default: Nifty 50 alone, or Nifty 500 alone. Optional split: about 70–80% Nifty 50 plus 20–30% Midcap 150 or Next 50 for those who accept more volatility.**

### Cited Findings
- **Concentration:** in Nifty 50 weights as of 19 Aug 2026, HDFC Bank is 10.01%, Bharti Airtel 5.30% and Axis Bank 3.22%. The full top 10 was cut off in the extract. — [Taurus MF Nifty constituents 19-08-2026](https://www.taurusmutualfund.com/sites/default/files/2026-08/nifty-index-constituent-as-on-19_08_2026.pdf)
- Nifty 50 is said to cover about 66% of NSE float-adjusted market cap (secondary blog, verify with NSE). — [India Macro Indicators](https://indiamacroindicators.co.in/resources/blogs/nifty-50-companies-list-2026-top-50-stocks-in-india)
- Nifty 500 covers about 92% of NSE free-float market cap and is market-cap weighted. Its maximum drawdown was −63.7% vs −59.5% for Nifty 50 (snippet only). — [Basunivesh](https://www.basunivesh.com/nifty-50-vs-nifty-500-index-fund-which-one-should-you-pick/)
- The Nifty LargeMidcap 250 drawdown was about −67% over the full period, vs −63.7% for Nifty 500 (snippet only). — [Basunivesh](https://www.basunivesh.com/nifty-500-multicap-502525-vs-nifty-500-which-is-best/)
- **Next 50 vs Midcap 150, rolling returns since 2005 (snippet only):** 1-year returns ranged from about +189% to −66% for Next 50 and from +163% to −68% for Midcap 150. Three-year rolling returns peaked at about 43% vs 39% and bottomed at −14% vs −15%. — [Basunivesh](https://www.basunivesh.com/nifty-next-50-vs-nifty-midcap-150/)
- Freefincal's Aug 2026 update says that over 10 years Next 50 has only just stayed ahead of Nifty 50, and its outperformance comes and goes (snippet only). — [Freefincal Aug 2026](https://freefincal.com/nifty-vs-nifty-next-50-vs-nifty-midcap-150-vs-nifty-smallcap-250-return-comparison-aug-2026/)
- Freefincal finds little difference in 10-year rolling standard deviation between Nifty 500 and LargeMidcap 250, and calls Next 50 riskier to hold (snippet only). — [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/can-i-invest-in-a-nifty-largemidcap-250-index-fund/)
- Nifty 500 Multicap 50:25:25 is a different, fixed-weight index (50% large, 25% mid, 25% small), not plain Nifty 500. — [Basunivesh](https://www.basunivesh.com/nifty-500-multicap-502525-vs-nifty-500-which-is-best/)

### Inferences
- **Nifty 50:** cheapest TERs, deepest ETF liquidity, longest track record. Downside: heavy in a few banks and large companies, and no mid-cap exposure.
- **Nifty 100:** Nifty 50 plus Next 50. It changes little vs Nifty 50 and has fewer cheap funds, so there is little reason to prefer it.
- **Nifty 500 / "Total Market":** one-fund diversification across large, mid and small caps in market-cap proportion, roughly 75–80% large cap (estimate, verify). Its TER is slightly higher than Nifty 50 (about 0.14–0.17% direct), its tracking error is slightly higher (it holds 500 stocks), and there are fewer liquid ETFs. A good "one and done" core.
- **Next 50 and Midcap 150:** higher swings, with past 1-year drops of −66% to −68%. Use them as a deliberate satellite only.
- For someone who already holds individual stocks (likely large caps) in a demat, Nifty 500 overlaps less with a large-cap stock book. A plain Nifty 50 core is still fine.

### Inferences
- **What to check (one line each):**
  - **TER:** yearly fee. Lower is better, but 0.05% vs 0.15% is small.
  - **Tracking error:** how much daily returns wobble vs the index. Under about 0.1% is good.
  - **Tracking difference:** how far the fund's 1–3 year return trails the index. This is the real cost and should be close to the TER.
  - **AUM:** prefer at least ₹1,000 crore for index funds.
  - **ETF daily traded value:** prefer ETFs where several crore trade daily and the bid-ask spread is 1–2 paise.
- **Red flags:**
  - AUM under about ₹100–500 crore, or a fund less than a year old (no record).
  - Tracking error above about 0.2%.
  - A "smart beta", equal-weight or 50:25:25 variant mistaken for the plain index.
  - An ETF trading more than about 0.25–0.5% above iNAV.
  - A regular plan bought by mistake.
  - The IDCW option instead of Growth.

## 5. Practical how-to, decision tree, and example shortlist

### Takeaway
Monthly SIP from a bank account: buy a Direct–Growth Nifty 50 or Nifty 500 index fund through a direct platform with a bank mandate. Lump sum held in a demat account: buy a liquid Nifty 50 ETF on the exchange with a limit order near iNAV, during calm market hours.

### Cited Findings
- iNAV is published with a 15-second lag for equity ETFs. AMCs show it on their websites, and it is the reference "fair price". — [WIRC-ICAI SEBI update](https://old.wirc-icai.org/images/law-updates/sebi-aug-2022.pdf)
- Market makers must quote two-way prices, which is what keeps liquid ETF prices near iNAV. — [Zerodha Fund House policy](https://assets.zerodhafundhouse.com/statutory-reports/other-disclosures/Policy%20on%20Market%20Making%20in%20ETFs.pdf)
- ETFs can be bought intraday with limit orders. — [NSE Nifty BeES](https://nseindia.com/products/content/equities/indices/prod_niftyBees.htm)
- Upstox's mutual fund section offers direct plans with no charges. — [Upstox invest](https://upstox.com/invest/)
- Minimum SIP is ₹100 at Navi Nifty 50. — [Kuvera](https://kuvera.in/mutual-funds/fund/navi-nifty-50-index-fund-direct-plan-growth--ESNIGD-GR)

### Inferences

**Step-by-step: monthly SIP in a direct index fund**
1. Pick a platform that sells direct plans: the AMC website, MF Central, Zerodha Coin, Kuvera, Groww, or the Upstox MF section the user already has. KYC is shared across all of them via the KRA, so an existing demat KYC usually works.
2. Search for the scheme and make sure the name reads "… Nifty 50 Index Fund – **Direct Plan – Growth**", not Regular and not IDCW.
3. On the scheme page or factsheet, check that TER is about 0.20% or less, AUM is large, and tracking error is low.
4. Choose SIP, then the amount (e.g., ₹10,000) and a date (e.g., 5th, a few days after salary).
5. Set up an autopay or e-mandate (NACH or UPI AutoPay) from your bank. A NACH mandate can take a few days to activate.
6. Confirm. Units are allotted at that day's NAV if money reaches the fund before the cut-off.
7. Once a year, download the CAS from CAMS/KFintech/MF Central. Confirm "Direct" with no ARN, and compare the fund's 1-year return with the index.

**Step-by-step: buying an ETF near iNAV (e.g., on Upstox)**
1. Search for the symbol (e.g., NIFTYBEES) on NSE, choose **Delivery** (CNC), not intraday.
2. Open the AMC's iNAV page for that ETF (e.g., Nippon's live iNAV) and note the iNAV per unit.
3. Look at the market depth: best buy and sell price. In a liquid ETF they should be within a few paise of each other and of iNAV.
4. Place a **Limit** order (never a Market order) at or just above the best sell price. It should be no more than about 0.1–0.2% above iNAV.
5. Trade between about 10:00 am and 3:00 pm. Avoid the first 15 minutes and the closing minutes, when spreads are wider.
6. If the price is well above iNAV (a premium), wait or use a different ETF, or use the index fund instead.
7. ETFs come in whole units only, so the investment equals units × price. Repeat monthly manually if you are using ETFs for a SIP.

**Equity core decision tree**
1. **If** you invest monthly from salary and do not want to watch prices, **then** use a **Direct–Growth Nifty 50 index fund** with TER around 0.2% or less via SIP. Use Nifty 500 instead if you want mid and small caps in one fund.
2. **If** you are putting a lump sum (roughly ₹50k or more) into your demat account and are comfortable placing limit orders, **then** buy a **liquid Nifty 50 ETF** (NIFTYBEES, SETFNIF50, NIFTYIETF, HDFCNIFTY class) at a price near iNAV.
3. **If** you want one fund and never want to rebalance, **then** choose **Nifty 500** (or Nifty 50). Do not mix many index funds.
4. **If** you want more growth and can stomach a 60–70% drop on part of the money, **then** add a **satellite of no more than 20–30% Midcap 150 or Next 50**, and keep 70–80% in Nifty 50.
5. **If** you hold regular-plan funds, **then** move new SIPs to direct now and switch old units in stages: start with units held over 12 months, stay within the ₹1.25 lakh yearly LTCG exemption, and avoid the exit-load period.
6. **If** a fund or ETF has small AUM, a tracking error above about 0.2%, a variant index (equal-weight or 50:25:25), or an ETF price above iNAV, **then** skip it. Good alternatives are plentiful.
