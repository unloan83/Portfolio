# Historical base rates for Indian equity investing, by holding period

> **Method note:** Every direct page fetch (WebFetch and curl) failed because of DNS errors or a 403 from the proxy. So all figures below come from search-engine extracts of the cited pages, not from reading the pages in full. Numbers were cross-checked across sources where possible. Treat single-source figures as "verify before publishing", and the [UNVERIFIED] tags as stronger warnings. Today is 15 Oct 2026.

## 1. Rolling-return probabilities (lumpsum): % positive, % above thresholds, worst and average returns by horizon

### Takeaway
Indian large-cap equity losing money gets less likely the longer you hold it:
- **1 year:** about 24–35% of windows lost money.
- **3 years:** about 6–15%.
- **5 years:** about 0.1–7%.
- **7 years:** 0–6%.
- **10 years:** 0–1%.
- **15 years:** 0%.

Beating 10% p.a. is a different story. It is only around 56% likely over 1 year and about 75% over 5 years. It reaches about 86–96% only at 10–15 years. "Positive" is near-certain long term, but "high returns" is not.

**Other Sensex studies**
- Quantum AMC, Sensex 1979–2014, monthly rolling. Share of negative windows:
  - 1Y: about 26%
  - 3Y: about 15%
  - 5Y: about 6%
  - 10Y: about 4%
  - 15Y: 0%
  - Source: [Quantum AMC (2014)](https://www.quantumamc.com/article/how-long-is-long-term-for-equities)
  - The 10Y figure (4%) is higher than HSBC's (about 1%) because the study ends in 2014, after the post-1992/1994 periods.
- HDFC MF (Investment Mantra, June 2024), Sensex since 1979, counted on calendar or financial years:
  - Positive periods: 31 of 45 one-year periods (69%), 36 of 43 three-year (84%), 38 of 41 five-year (93%).
  - "Just one instance of negative return" in 10-year periods; none in 15-year or 20-year periods.
  - Sensex TRI CAGR from 1 Apr 1979 to 31 Mar 2024 was 15.79%.
  - Source: [HDFC MF Investment Mantra Jun 2024](https://files.hdfcfund.com/s3fs-public/2024-06/Investment%20Mantra%20%28June%202024%29.pdf); [HDFC MF article](https://www.hdfcfund.com/learn/deep-dives/weekend-bytes/pained-short-term-trades-welcome-long-term-investing)
- Business Today, entries on the first trading day, 1990–2018: probability of loss was 25% at 1Y, 16.7% at 5Y and 4.55% at 7Y. — [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/magazine/investment/story/minimising-loss-16669-2010-04-24)
- DSIJ, rolling returns from 1990: about 65% chance of gain at 1Y and about 96% at 10Y. — [DSIJ](https://insights.dsij.in/article-details/articleid/8897/equity-investments-the-time-returns-relation)
- arXiv study of the Nifty 50, daily data 1990–2024: 74% probability of a positive 1-year return. — [arXiv 2509.00697](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00697v1)

### Inferences
- The studies broadly agree:
  - 1-year loss probability is about 24–35%. That makes it roughly a 3:1 or 2:1 bet on being positive, which is better than a coin flip but far from safe.
  - The 3-year loss probability is 6–15%.
  - From 7 years on, the loss probability is at most about 6% (0% in the 1999+ TRI data).
  - From 10 years on, it is at most about 1% (0% in the TRI data).
  - From 15 years on, it is 0% in every dataset.
- The TRI-based data (1999 onward) shows fewer losses than the price-index data from 1979. That is partly because dividends add about 1–1.5% p.a., and partly because of the sample period.
- The worst 10Y TRI CAGR was about 5%. That is below typical FD rates of about 6.5–9%, so "positive" at 10Y does not mean "beat FD" at 10Y. The ">10%" probability of about 86% is a reasonable proxy for "comfortably beat FD/inflation".
- Overlapping windows make these percentages look more precise than they are. 27 years of data contains fewer than three independent 10-year periods.

### Gaps
- No source found gives a direct "% of rolling windows > CPI inflation" or "> FD rate" figure for the Sensex or Nifty TRI. You would need to compute it from the TRI series plus CPI data and the RBI's FD rate series.
- Medians are not published in any source found; only averages, minimums and maximums.
- I could not confirm the FundsIndia column order directly from the PDF because the fetch failed.
- The full-period worst rolling 1Y/3Y/5Y figures for the Sensex since 1979 were not found.

## 2. Probability that equity beat FD, debt or gold over rolling 5/10/15 years

### Takeaway
No reliable Indian study found gives a quantified probability that equity beat FDs over rolling periods. The evidence is qualitative or covers a single period. Equity beat gold in most rolling periods longer than 3 years, but gold has led over some 15–20-year stretches.

### Cited Findings
- An ICICI Pru MF analysis of 1996–2016 found that both the Sensex and gold "beat FD comfortably over a 10 year investment horizon". Sensex and gold each led in different periods, and the Sensex won by bigger margins when it led. — [Advisorkhoj / ICICI Pru MF](https://www.advisorkhoj.com/iciciprumf/Equity-is-the-best-performing-asset-class-in-long-term:-Myth-or-Truth)
- A Business Today rolling-returns analysis of more than 3,100 ten-year holding periods (Oct 2025) found that the Nifty 50 "consistently beat gold for all durations longer than three years". — [Business Today Oct 2025](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/investment/story/you-loaded-up-on-gold-this-diwali-but-the-real-winners-been-hiding-in-plain-sight-498994-2025-10-21)
- Motilal Oswal reported average rolling returns for the Sensex of 14.63% (5Y) and 13.55% (10Y), against 10.28% and 9.85% for gold. The periods were not specified. — [Motilal Oswal](https://www.motilaloswal.com/news/sensex/8960)
- Freefincal found a period when 20-year Sensex returns fell below gold's. It also said no one can confidently claim that equity outperforms most of the time over 10 years. — [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/20-year-sensex-returns-now-lower-than-gold-stocks-vs-gold-vs-bonds/)
- Moneylife argued that a widely quoted "FD beat Sensex over 20 years" claim depended on the start date: starting a year earlier or later would have reversed the result. — [Moneylife](https://www.moneylife.in/article/fixed-deposits-beats-stocks-financial-writers-need-financial-literacy-first/24946.html)
- In FundsIndia's Oct 2026 report, the Nifty 50 TRI was down 7.1% over the past year to Sep 2026. Over 10 and 15 years, US equities led the asset-class table and real estate came last. — via [Livemint/alpha-maven summary](https://alpha-maven.com/story/real-estate/mutual-fund-investors-nifty-50-fell-71-in-a-year-could-us-equities-gold-and-debt-cushion-portfolio-losses-in-2026-livemint)

### Inferences
- Section 1 shows about 86% of 10Y Nifty TRI windows above 10% CAGR, against pre-tax FD rates of roughly 6–9% over 1999–2026. From this, the probability that equity beat FDs over 10 years is very likely at least about 85–90%, and over 15 years about 95–100%. This is an inference, not a measured figure.
- Over 5 years, about 75% of windows returned more than 10%. That implies equity beat FDs in very roughly 75–85% of 5-year windows (also inferred).
- Equity vs gold is genuinely mixed over 10–20 year windows. Gold surged in 2001–2011 and again in 2024–2026.

### Gaps
- No primary study found gives "% of rolling N-year windows where Sensex/Nifty TRI > SBI FD / RBI deposit rate". This is the most important missing number.
- No rolling comparison was found against a debt index (for example the CRISIL Composite Bond index).

## 3. SIP outcomes: % of rolling 5/7/10-year Nifty SIPs with positive XIRR; worst SIP XIRR

### Takeaway
For Nifty 50 TRI SIPs from June 1999 to June 2026, no 7-year SIP ended negative. 10-year SIPs have never been negative, but they have dropped as low as about 2–4% XIRR (March 2020, price index). Over 1–3 years, SIPs can lose heavily.

### Cited Findings
- FundsIndia studied every rolling Nifty 50 TRI SIP from June 1999 to June 2026:
  - No 7-year SIP "ended in the red".
  - 43 seven-year periods returned less than 10%.
  - In 31 of those 43, returns had recovered above 10% by the 8th year.
  - Source: [FundsIndia Substack](https://fundsindia.substack.com/p/does-a-7-year-horizon-hold-the-key)
- Freefincal, Nifty price index: a 10-year SIP valued on 23 March 2020 had an XIRR of 2.33%. A 10-year SIP from 1 Apr 2010 to 3 Apr 2020 had 3.4%, and Freefincal called this an all-time low. — [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/?p=33478); [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/nifty-sip-returns/)
- Freefincal reported a 15-year Nifty SIP return of about 8% in that crash period. — [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/?p=33678)
- Capitalmind, 2017: 10-year Nifty SIP returns were about 7%. — [Capitalmind](https://premium.capitalmind.in/2017/10/charts-10-year-sip-returns-nifty-7-marginally-negative-september/)
- A FundsIndia study, cited second-hand, gives worst SIP returns of about -65% over 1 year, about -40% over 2 years and about -22% over 3 years [UNVERIFIED: second-hand via CB Insights; probably absolute returns rather than XIRR]. — [CB Insights page](https://www.cbinsights.com/company/wealth-india-financial-services/alternatives-competitors)
- Freefincal's long-run SIP study (Sensex price + 2% dividend proxy) found that 15-year SIPs "comfortably" beat CPI and PPF, "although not always". — [Freefincal 110-year analysis](https://freefincal.com/does-long-term-equity-sip-investing-work-110-year-analysis/)

### Inferences
- A 7-year or longer SIP had a positive XIRR in 100% of observed cases (TRI, 1999–2026). Getting at least 10% XIRR after 7 years is not certain: at least 43 windows fell short.
- The worst 10-year SIP (about 2–3% on the price index, perhaps about 3.5–4.5% on TRI) was below FD returns. That makes "SIP beats FD" over 10 years a high-probability outcome, not a certain one.

### Gaps
- No exact "% of rolling 5Y/10Y SIPs with positive XIRR" table was found. The worst 5Y SIP XIRR on TRI was also not found.

## 4. Drawdown base rates

### Takeaway
Intra-year falls of at least 10% are the norm in India: about 89% of calendar years since 1980 had one (Sensex). Falls of at least 20% happen about once every 3–4 years, and falls of 30–60% roughly once every 7–10 years. Recovery to the previous peak has taken from under 1 year to about 3 years (2008: 1,032 days).

### Cited Findings
- FundsIndia, Sensex 1980–2026:
  - Only 5 of 46 calendar years had an intra-year fall under 10%, so about 89% of years saw a fall of at least 10%.
  - The average intra-year decline was about 20%.
  - Falls of 30–60% came about once every 7–10 years.
  - Recovery took "over two years on average".
  - Source: [FundsIndia Substack](https://fundsindia.substack.com/p/the-psychology-of-staying-invested); [FundsIndia blog](https://www.fundsindia.com/blog/mf-basics/investment-definitions/indian-equity-markets-decline-what-should-you-do/34341)
- An older FundsIndia analysis of 29 years of Nifty data:
  - Average intra-year decline was -22%.
  - Corrections of 10–30% occurred in 20 of 29 years.
  - Despite this, 21 of 29 calendar years ended positive.
  - Source: [FundsIndia blog](https://www.fundsindia.com/blog/mf-research/how-to-make-the-most-of-stock-market-declines/16305)
- Abakkus (via CafeMutual):
  - Nifty corrections of 10–20% happened 13 times, about once every 2.7 years.
  - Falls of more than 20% happened 9 times, about once every 4 years.
  - Recovery after the 2008 fall of 59.9% took 1,032 days. The Covid fall of 38.4% completed its fall and recovery in 300 days.
  - Source: [CafeMutual](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/38496-nifty-50-needs-8-gain-to-reclaim-its-peak-abakkus-mutual-fund)
- Arthgyaan, Nifty since 1992, with drawdown episodes measured against the trailing 12-month high: 59 episodes below -10%, 32 below -20%, 15 below -30% and 6 below -40%. — [Arthgyaan](https://arthgyaan.com/blog/dealing-with-stock-market-corrections.html)
- Freefincal, Nifty since July 1990:
  - Falls of more than 20% ("crashes") happened 10 times, with "no pattern" in the gap between them.
  - About 45% of 15% drawdowns (1,540 of 3,453 instances) went on to become 30% drawdowns, which Freefincal calls "close to a coin toss".
  - Source: [Freefincal](https://freefincal.com/nifty-crash/)
  - The extract also reported time-at-depth shares that look internally inconsistent [UNVERIFIED].
- NISM counts 8 Nifty bear markets (falls of at least 20%) since the 1990s, with an average decline of about 39%. — [NISM](https://www.nism.ac.in/?p=49548)
- Wright Research (Mar 2026) covers 12 major Nifty drawdown episodes since 1990:
  - "Event shock" falls averaged 34.3% and lasted about 5 months, followed by fast recoveries.
  - The Nifty peaked at 26,373 on 5 Jan 2026 and was down about 14.1% by 20 Mar 2026.
  - Source: [Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/blog/what-history-tells-us-about-indian-stock-market-corrections/)
- FundsIndia found 13 Nifty drawdowns of more than 10% in 20 years. 12 had fully recovered; one, which began in January 2026, was still ongoing. — via [FundsIndia](https://fundsindia.substack.com/p/how-to-exit-an-equity-sip-when-youre)

### Inferences
- The chance of a fall of at least 10% at some point within a given year is about 85–90%, which makes it close to certain. The chance of a fall of at least 20% in a given year is about 25–30% (about 9–10 events in roughly 35 years).
- Per decade, that works out to about 3 falls of at least 20% and about 1–1.5 falls of at least 30%. Falls of at least 10% happen roughly every year.
- Once the market is down 15%, the chance it goes on to fall 30% is about 45%: a genuine coin flip.

### Gaps
- No single source gives a consistent table of recovery times for falls of 10%, 20% and 30%. The figures here (300 days to about 3 years for large falls) come from individual episodes.

## 5. Day-to-day and month-to-month direction

### Takeaway
About 56% of Nifty days were up (1990–2024), rising to about 74% of 1-year windows. Short-term direction is barely better than a coin flip.

### Cited Findings
- Nifty 50, daily data from 1 Jan 1990 to 31 Dec 2024: 56% of days were positive, with a reward-risk ratio of 1.26. 74% of 1-year returns were positive. — [arXiv 2509.00697](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.00697v1)
- US comparison: 53.7% of S&P 500 days were up over 1951–2025. — [Crestmont Research](https://www.crestmontresearch.com/docs/Stock-Yo-Yo.pdf)
- Calendar years: 21 of 29 years were positive (Nifty, about 72%), per [FundsIndia](https://www.fundsindia.com/blog/mf-research/how-to-make-the-most-of-stock-market-declines/16305). HDFC MF counts 31 of 45 positive years (Sensex since 1979, about 69%) in [HDFC MF](https://www.hdfcfund.com/learn/deep-dives/weekend-bytes/pained-short-term-trades-welcome-long-term-investing).

### Inferences
- The probability of a positive result climbs with the horizon: about 53–56% for a day, about 60% for a month (estimated), about 70–76% for a year, about 94% for 3 years (TRI) and 100% for 7 years or more (TRI).

### Gaps
- No source found gives the % of positive months for the Nifty or Sensex. My estimate of about 58–62% is an interpolation [UNVERIFIED]. It could be computed from NSE data.

## 6. Missing the best days

### Takeaway
Missing only the 10 best days out of about 5,000 trading days cut the final value of a Nifty 500 investment by about half over roughly 20 years. Missing the 15 best days over 25 years cut the final value by about two-thirds. The best days tend to cluster inside or right after crashes.

### Cited Findings
- Zerodha Varsity, Nifty 500, about 20 years:
  - Missing the 10 best days of about 5,000 trading days left a final value of ₹9 lakh, 52% less than staying invested throughout.
  - About 40 of the 60 best days fell during or shortly after a correction.
  - Source: [Zerodha Varsity](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/varsity/what-happens-if-you-miss-some-of-the-best-or-worst-days-in-the-market)
- FundsIndia, Nifty 50 TRI since 2005: "If you missed the 15 best days in the last 25+ years, your portfolio lost 2/3rd of its value." Many of the best days occurred during the worst declines. — [FundsIndia Insights Dec 2021](https://www.fundsindia.com/blog/wp-content/uploads/2021/12/202112-FundsIndia-Insights.pdf)
- CAIA, Nifty measured in USD over about 23–24 years: the base return was about 8.4% p.a. Missing the 10 best days cut it to 4.7%, and missing the 30 best days cut it to 0%. — [CAIA](https://caia.org/node/7206)
- Stable Investor (blog), Sensex over about 40 years: ₹44,000 fell to ₹15,000 when the 10 best days were missed. — [Stable Investor](https://stableinvestor.com/2022/07/miss-10-best-days-40-years-markets.html)

### Inferences
- Because best days cluster near the worst days, a market-timing exit during a crash carries a large expected cost. That cost is concentrated in roughly 0.2% of trading days.

### Gaps
- No Indian figure was found for missing the 20 best days specifically. No study found tested missing the best and worst days together.

**Caveats:**
- All figures are historical. Rolling windows overlap, so a 10Y "100%" over 1999–2026 rests on fewer than three non-overlapping decades.
- Price-index studies understate returns by the dividend yield (about 1–1.5% p.a.).
- No study found gives a measured probability of equity beating FDs or inflation; that remains the key gap.
