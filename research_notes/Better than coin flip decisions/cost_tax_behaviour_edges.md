# Cost, tax, diversification and behaviour edges for Indian investors: decisions that win with near-certainty

Research notes, compiled 15 Oct 2026. Scope: India-focused decisions whose payoff comes from arithmetic (costs, taxes), from diversification, or from avoiding known behavioural losses, not from forecasting. Method note: spglobal.com, marcellus.in and arthgyaan.com could not be fetched directly from this environment (DNS/proxy blocks). SPIVA figures therefore come from search-engine extracts of the S&P pages and PDFs. They should be checked against the PDFs before publication. Arithmetic illustrations were computed by the researcher and are labelled "computed".

## 1. Index funds vs active funds (SPIVA India, persistence)

### Takeaway
On S&P's year-end 2025 SPIVA India data, most active large-cap funds trailed the benchmark over every horizon: 75.0% (1 year), 74.2% (3 years), 84.4% (5 years) and 76.3% (10 years). A majority in every category underperformed over the 10 years to December 2025. Mid/small-cap funds were the exception over the shorter horizons in 2025. Choosing a low-cost index fund over a randomly chosen active large-cap fund has roughly a 3-in-4 to 5-in-6 chance of doing better. The edge is weaker, and less certain, in mid/small-cap over short horizons.

### Cited Findings
- **SPIVA India year-end 2025, large-cap:** underperformance was 75.0% (1 year), 74.2% (3 years), 84.4% (5 years) and 76.3% (10 years). The S&P India LargeMidCap rose 8.9% in 2025. Large-cap funds returned 7.3% equal-weighted and 9.4% asset-weighted. — [S&P DJI, SPIVA India Year-End 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india-year-end-2025/); [PDF](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-india-scorecard-year-end-2025.pdf)
- **SPIVA year-end 2025, ELSS:** 69.2% of ELSS funds underperformed the S&P India BMI in 2025; the index returned 6.3%. — [S&P DJI, SPIVA India Year-End 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india-year-end-2025/)
- **SPIVA year-end 2025, mid/small-cap:** a majority of active mid/small-cap funds outperformed in 2025, their best relative result since 2014. A firm majority of funds in every category still underperformed over the decade ending December 2025. Government bond funds had the highest underperformance rate of any category. — [S&P DJI, SPIVA India Year-End 2025](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/spiva-india-year-end-2025/)
- **SPIVA mid-year 2025 (to June 2025), the latest category-by-horizon figures retrieved:**
  - ELSS underperformance: 35.9% (1 year), 52.6% (3 years), 68.3% (5 years), 86.8% (10 years).
  - Mid/small-cap underperformance: 38.6% (1 year), 80.0% (3 years), 67.3% (5 years), 81.7% (10 years).
  - 55% of government bond funds were merged or liquidated over 10 years.
  - Source: [S&P DJI, SPIVA India Mid-Year 2025 PDF](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/documents/spiva/spiva-india-scorecard-mid-year-2025.pdf)
- **Conflicting secondary figures:** Cafemutual (Oct 2025) reported 73% of large-cap and 82% of mid/small-cap funds underperforming over 10 years, and 90% over 5 years. These come from an earlier scorecard and do not match the year-end 2025 figures, so the S&P primary figures should be used. — [Cafemutual](https://cafemutual.com/news/industry/35917-73-of-indian-large-cap-and-82-of-mid-and-small-cap-funds-have-underperformed-sp-india-benchmarks-over-a-10-year-period)
- **Persistence, other markets (no India scorecard found):**
  - US, year-end 2024: of the top-quartile domestic equity funds at December 2020, none stayed in the top quartile in each of the next four years. — [S&P DJI, US Persistence Scorecard](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/us-persistence-scorecard)
  - Australia, year-end 2019: 14.1% of top-quartile funds stayed top-quartile over consecutive 3-year periods, and 1.0% over 5-year periods. — [S&P DJI Australia Persistence YE2019](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/australia-persistence-scorecard-year-end-2019)
  - Europe, year-end 2020: 41.3% of top-quartile funds repeated across two consecutive 3-year windows, and 42.0% across 5-year windows. A random 1-in-4 draw would give 25%. — [S&P DJI Europe Persistence YE2020](https://www.spglobal.com/spdji/en/spiva/article/europe-persistence-scorecard-year-end-2020)

### Inferences
- The asset-weighted large-cap return (9.4%) beat the index (8.9%) in 2025 while the equal-weighted return (7.3%) lagged it. Over one year, the outcome depends on which funds an investor happens to hold. Over 5 and 10 years the large-cap underperformance rate stays at 75–85%, which is the near-certain part.
- The index-fund edge is strongest in large-cap and ELSS (10-year underperformance of 76–87%). It is weaker and time-varying in mid/small-cap, where 1-year underperformance was below 50% in 2025. Over 10 years, mid/small-cap was still about 82% (mid-year 2025).
- Survivorship matters. Funds that are merged or liquidated count as underperformers in SPIVA. Picking an active fund carries extra risk that the fund disappears.
- In other markets, top-quartile persistence over 5 years is at or below what chance would give. Past top performance therefore says little about the next 5 years. This is an inference from US, Australian and European data and has not been verified for India.

### Gaps
- No SPIVA India Persistence Scorecard was found; S&P does not appear to publish one for India. India-specific top-quartile persistence remains unverified.
- Year-end 2025 figures at 3, 5 and 10 years for ELSS, mid/small-cap and bond categories were not retrievable because the PDF fetch was blocked. Only the mid-year 2025 values above are available.

## 2. Direct vs regular plans

### Takeaway
The direct plan of the same scheme holds the same portfolio and differs only by a lower expense ratio, so it beats the regular plan with essentially 100% probability (before any value of advice). The typical equity gap is about 0.5–1.1 percentage points a year. That compounds to roughly 8–16% less terminal wealth over 20 years.

### Cited Findings
- **Expense gaps:**
  - Equity-oriented schemes: ACE MF data for April 2025 put the direct-vs-regular gap at 30–190 bps. — [Value Research](https://www.valueresearchonline.com/stories/47004/direct-plans-are-hugely-underappreciated/) (source of the ACE MF number per the search extract; verify)
  - Average diversified equity fund: about 1.12% for direct plans and about 2.07% for regular plans, a gap of roughly 0.95 points. The underlying data source is not stated. — [Kuvera](https://kuvera.in/blog/?p=42405)
  - Active equity funds: a typical gap of about 0.5–1.1% a year. — [EquitiesIndia](https://equitiesindia.com/learn/mutual-funds/direct-vs-regular-plans)
- **Illustrative 20-year SIP of ₹10,000/month:**
  - Kuvera: about ₹99.9 lakh (direct) vs ₹87.4 lakh (regular), assuming 12% vs 11%; a gap of about ₹12.5 lakh. — [Kuvera](https://kuvera.in/blog/?p=42405)
  - OnePercentClub: about ₹93.4 lakh vs ₹81.76 lakh. — [OnePercentClub](https://www.onepercentclub.io/blog/?p=1635)
- **Switching cost:** moving existing regular-plan units to direct counts as a redemption, which triggers capital gains tax (and exit load, if any). — [Kuvera](https://kuvera.in/blog/?p=42405)
- **Computed, lump sum at a 12% gross return:**
  - Terminal-wealth shortfall from a 0.5-point gap: 4.4% (10 years), 6.5% (15 years), 8.6% (20 years).
  - From a 0.75-point gap: 6.5% / 9.6% / 12.6%.
  - From a 1.0-point gap: 8.6% / 12.6% / 16.4%.
- **Computed, ₹10,000/month SIP for 20 years at a 12% gross return:**

  | Gap | Direct corpus | Regular corpus | Shortfall |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 0.75 point | ≈ ₹92.0 lakh | ≈ ₹84.0 lakh | ≈ ₹7.9 lakh |
  | 1.0 point | ≈ ₹92.0 lakh | ≈ ₹81.6 lakh | ≈ ₹10.4 lakh |

### Inferences
- For a do-it-yourself investor the probability that direct beats regular in the same scheme is effectively 100%: same portfolio, lower total expense ratio. The only offset is the value of a distributor's advice, including behavioural hand-holding (see section 3).
- When switching existing holdings, it may be better to wait for units to cross 12 months and to use the ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption, rather than switching all at once.

### Gaps
- No AMFI or SEBI official average of the direct-regular total expense ratio gap by category for 2025 was retrieved. Category averages above come from platforms (Kuvera, EquitiesIndia). The ACE MF figure is second-hand.

## 3. Behaviour gap (investor vs fund returns, SIP stoppage, F&O and intraday losses)

### Takeaway
Indian studies consistently find that investors earn 1.3–6 points a year less than the funds they hold, because of badly timed flows. Staying invested, or automating SIPs and not stopping them, captures part of this gap with high probability. SEBI's own data show that 70%+ of intraday traders and about 91–93% of F&O traders lose money. Avoiding these activities is a near-certain gain for the median retail participant.

### Cited Findings
- **Axis MF study (2003–2020):** average equity MF investor return of about 13.2% a year, against 14.5% for equity SIPs and 18.7% for the funds themselves. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pf/returns-made-by-average-mf-investor-lag-those-of-schemes-and-sips-study-121040101236_1.html); [Axis MF PDF](https://transact.axismf.com/cms/sites/default/files/pdf-factsheets/Are-investors-creating-enough-wealth-from-their-MF-investments.pdf)
  - A later 2003–2022 version reports a 19.1% fund CAGR against 13.8% for investors. — [Business Standard / ANI](https://www.business-standard.com/content/press-releases-ani/decoding-wealth-creation-expert-analysis-on-the-behaviour-gap-in-investment-returns-vs-investor-returns-by-santosh-kedari-ceo-sanriya-finvest-123082900013_1.html) (second-hand; verify)
- **Morningstar India (data to June 2022):** the gap between investor returns (money-weighted) and fund returns (time-weighted) was 2.7 points (3 years), 2.5 points (5 years) and 5.8 points (10 years) across the MF universe. The gap was mainly due to poorly timed entry and exit, and smaller in well-diversified categories such as large-cap. — [Business Standard, Oct 2022](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pf/reduce-behaviour-gap-with-higher-exposure-to-diversified-funds-122102600668_1.html)
- **Value Research (unverified):** a 3.21% annual behaviour gap across 170 diversified equity funds over 10 years, cited second-hand. — [Tradejini blog](https://www-tradejini-com.translate.goog/blogs/why-investor-returns-trail-fund-performance?_x_tr_sl=en&_x_tr_tl=hi&_x_tr_hl=hi&_x_tr_pto=tc)

  - 76% of traders under 30 lost money.
  - Intraday participation was up more than 300% vs FY19.

- **Vanguard Advisor's Alpha (US framework):**
  - Behavioural coaching is estimated at about 150 bps a year on average, with recent editions saying "up to 2%"; the total adviser value-add is about 3 points. — [InvestmentNews](https://investmentnews.com/practice-management/advisers-can-add-3-percentage-points-to-clients-net-returns/56824); [Vanguard Canada infographic](https://vanguard.ca/content/dam/intl/americas/canada/en/documents/gas/advisors-alpha-infographic.pdf)
  - Vanguard stresses that the value is lumpy: concentrated in panic periods, not earned evenly each year.

### Inferences
- SEBI's intraday finding (more trades among loss-makers, costs adding 57% on top of losses) is direct Indian evidence for the Barber–Odean "trading is hazardous to your wealth" result. It is an inference that lower churn helps, not a controlled study of it.
- The behaviour gap (about 1.3–6 points a year) is larger than the direct-vs-regular gap (about 1 point). A regular plan that keeps an investor from panic-selling could, in principle, be worth more than its cost. For a disciplined investor, direct plus automation captures both gains.
- For the median retail participant, avoiding F&O is a decision with about a 90% chance of improving outcomes, using SEBI's measured loss share as the probability. The same reasoning gives about 70% for avoiding intraday trading. These are population base rates, not individual forecasts.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed Indian study of the behaviour gap was found; the evidence comes from AMC and research-house reports, which may have commercial interests.
- No Indian Barber–Odean-style academic paper linking portfolio turnover to returns among Indian retail equity investors was retrieved; this needs a follow-up search, e.g. Anagol, Balasubramaniam and Ramadorai on Indian investors.
- AMFI average holding period by investor category was not retrieved.

## 4. Tax decisions (LTCG harvesting, holding past 12 months, tax-loss harvesting)

### Takeaway
Under the post-23 July 2024 regime, Indian equity gains are taxed at 12.5% LTCG above a ₹1.25 lakh annual exemption if the holding is more than 12 months, and at 20% STCG otherwise. Waiting past 12 months for a gain that is close to the threshold, harvesting ₹1.25 lakh of gains each year, and booking losses to offset gains all save tax with near-certainty. The amounts are modest in absolute terms but risk-free.

### Rules
These are the researcher's knowledge of the Finance (No. 2) Act 2024. No primary source was fetched in this session, and the report-writer should cite the Income Tax Department or CBDT.
- Listed equity and equity-oriented mutual funds: STCG (holding of 12 months or less) is taxed at 20%. LTCG (holding over 12 months) is taxed at 12.5% on gains above ₹1.25 lakh per financial year.
- Short-term capital losses can be set off against both STCG and LTCG. Long-term capital losses can be set off only against LTCG. Both can be carried forward for 8 years if the return is filed on time.
- No wash-sale rule exists in Indian law as of the researcher's knowledge. Repurchasing immediately after harvesting is therefore permitted, but this needs verification against current law.

### Computed illustrations
- **Holding past 12 months:**
  - A ₹1 lakh gain costs ₹20,000 in tax as STCG and ₹0 as LTCG, because it falls within the exemption if no other LTCG is booked that year.
  - A ₹5 lakh gain costs ₹1,00,000 as STCG and ₹46,875 as LTCG, a saving of ₹53,125.
  - The probability that waiting improves after-tax outcomes is not 100%: the price can fall during the wait. When only a few weeks remain, though, the tax saving of 7.5 to 20 points of the gain usually far exceeds the expected price risk over that window.
- **Annual LTCG harvesting:** sell and rebuy to realise ₹1.25 lakh of gains each year, stepping up the cost basis.
  - The maximum saving is ₹15,625 per person per year (12.5% of ₹1.25 lakh).
  - Over 20 years this is up to ₹3.1 lakh nominal per person, and twice that for a couple holding separately.
  - Computed for a lump sum at 12% for 20 years, with all remaining gains realised at the end:
    - ₹5 lakh invested: about ₹2.8 lakh more after tax.
    - ₹10 lakh invested: about ₹3.1 lakh more.
    - ₹25 lakh invested: about ₹3.1 lakh more.
  - For large corpuses the benefit is capped and therefore small in percentage terms: about 1.4% of terminal value for ₹25 lakh invested.
  - Friction costs to account for: stamp duty and STT on equity, exit loads, and a possible 1–2 day out-of-market gap for mutual funds.
- **Tax-loss harvesting:** a ₹1 lakh short-term loss booked against ₹1 lakh of STCG saves ₹20,000. Booked against LTCG above the exemption, it saves ₹12,500.

### Inferences
- These tax moves are "certain" in the sense that the tax saving is arithmetic. The net benefit depends on transaction costs, exit loads and tracking during the time out of the market.
- Harvesting is most valuable for small and mid-sized portfolios relative to their size, because the benefit is capped per person.

### Gaps
- The tax rules above were not verified against a primary source in this session.
- Whether the Section 87A rebate applies to special-rate capital gains has been contested. Budget 2025 and court rulings should be checked before relying on it.
- No Indian data were found on how many investors actually use LTCG harvesting.

## 5. Diversification (single stocks vs index; Bessembinder evidence for India)

### Takeaway
Globally, most individual stocks underperform cash over their lifetimes, and a small minority create all the net wealth: 57.4% of non-US stocks trailed one-month US T-bills over 1990–2020. No India-specific Bessembinder replication was found. The best India-specific data point found is that 56% of more than 2,800 listed Indian companies returned less than a Post Office deposit over the 3 years to September 2026, on price returns only. A diversified index therefore beats the typical, or median, single stock with well above 50% probability.

### Cited Findings
- **Bessembinder et al., global study (64,000+ stocks, 1990/91–2020):**
  - 55.2% of US stocks and 57.4% of non-US stocks underperformed one-month US T-bills in compound returns.
  - The top 2.4% of firms accounted for all US$75.7 trillion of net global wealth creation.
  - Sources: [ASU W.P. Carey](https://wpcarey.asu.edu/department-finance/faculty-research/do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills); [Morningstar Australia](https://www.morningstar.com.au/insights/stocks/233587/only-24-of-companies-deliver-all-net-shareholder-wealth)
  - An earlier version covering about 62,000 stocks over 1990–2018 found the top 1.3% created US$44.7 trillion. — [Alpha Architect](https://alphaarchitect.com/do-most-individual-stocks-outperform-cash-no/)
  - A related 2021 study across 57 countries found that a larger share of stocks underperform where governance is weaker. — per the search extract; full citation not retrieved
- **US original (CRSP data from 1926):**
  - About 4% of stocks account for all the net wealth creation.
  - The median lifetime buy-and-hold return was −2.29%.
  - Source: [ASU, "Do stocks outperform Treasury bills?"](https://knowledge.wpcarey.asu.edu/sites/default/files/do-stocks-outperform-treasury-bills.pdf)
- **India, 3-year window:** 56% of more than 2,800 listed companies (1,595 firms) returned less than a Post Office deposit over the 3 years to 17 Sept 2026, on a price-only basis. — [Whalesbook](https://www.whalesbook.com/news/English/stock-investment-ideas/Indias-Bull-Market-Trails-Fixed-Deposits-3-Year-Data/6aaeb5ac629e4186971b64d3) (secondary; methodology unverified)
- **Counterpoint, survivor-biased:** Goldman Sachs found over half of NSE-500 stocks delivered 10x returns over 20 years. This counts only current index members, so it excludes failures. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/over-half-of-nse-500-stocks-deliver-10x-return-in-20-years-goldman-sachs-123060500276_1.html)
- **Wealth destruction (Motilal Oswal Wealth Creation Study 2018–23):**
  - Destroyed wealth totalled ₹17.31 lakh crore.
  - Financials were the largest wealth-destroying sector.
  - Vodafone Idea, YES Bank, IOCL, Indiabulls Housing and IndusInd Bank were the top five destroyers.
  - Source: [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/markets/stock-picks/story/these-are-the-biggest-wealth-destroying-stocks-of-2018-2023-410361-2023-12-21)

### Inferences
- Positive skew means the median stock underperforms the average stock, and the average is roughly the index. A single stock or a 5–10 stock portfolio is therefore more likely than not to trail the index over 10 years, even before stock-picking skill is considered. The exact probability for India over 10 years is not established.
- The Goldman finding (over half of NSE-500 stocks gave 10x in 20 years) and the Bessembinder findings are consistent once survivorship is accounted for. Indian bull-market periods can also make individual stocks look better than they are over the long run.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed India replication of Bessembinder (share of NSE/BSE stocks beating FDs or T-bills over their lifetimes) was found. The global paper's country appendix may contain India; it was not checked. The Marcellus "Extreme Returns" note may have India data but could not be fetched.
- No India-specific estimate was found of the probability that an N-stock portfolio trails the index over 10 years. A Monte Carlo study on NSE data would be needed.

## 6. Rebalancing

### Takeaway
Rebalancing keeps portfolio risk near the target allocation with near-certainty, which is a mechanical result. Its effect on return is uncertain and can be negative in trending markets. Vanguard estimates the value of disciplined rebalancing at roughly 0–50 bps a year in risk-adjusted terms, depending on the edition.

### Cited Findings
- **Vanguard Advisor's Alpha, rebalancing component:** estimates vary by edition and reproduction: 35 bps (secondary summary), 0–51 bps (Architas reproduction of the Vanguard framework), and 0–43 bps (Portfolio Adviser table). The components do not apply to every client. — [Architas/Vanguard presentation](https://select.axa-im.com/globalassets/inside-architas/presentations/the-changing-role-of-advisers-vanguard.pdf); [Portfolio Adviser](https://portfolio-adviser.com/?p=6042); [PlanAdviser](https://www.planadviser.com/vanguard-touts-five-ways-to-boost-returns/)

### Inferences
- In Vanguard's framing, the value of rebalancing comes mainly from risk control, i.e. avoiding drift into a riskier equity weight. It is not a return forecast. Its probability of "keeping risk on target" is close to 100%, while its probability of "adding return" is near a coin flip or uncertain.
- In India, each rebalance outside tax-advantaged wrappers can create STCG or LTCG tax. Rebalancing through fresh flows (SIP redirection) or within the ₹1.25 lakh LTCG exemption keeps the risk benefit without the tax drag.

### Gaps
- No India-specific rebalancing study (e.g., Nifty vs gilt or debt 60/40 with annual rebalancing) was retrieved. Freefincal and Capitalmind have published such backtests, but they were not fetched.

## 7. Emergency fund and avoiding forced selling

### Takeaway
No Indian quantitative study was found that measures outcomes of forced selling. The argument rests on arithmetic and behavioural evidence. Being forced to sell after a drawdown locks in losses and misses recoveries. Indian behaviour-gap evidence shows badly timed exits are the main source of the 1.3–6 point annual gap. An emergency fund makes such forced sales less likely.

### Cited Findings
- Morningstar India attributes the investor return gap mainly to poorly timed entry and exit. — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/article/pf/reduce-behaviour-gap-with-higher-exposure-to-diversified-funds-122102600668_1.html)
- The SIP stoppage ratio spiked to 109–128% in January–March 2025 as markets corrected. Investors cut flows during the drawdown, which is the opposite of buying low. This is partly a data artefact (see section 3). — [BusinessToday](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/mutual-funds/story/sip-stoppage-ratio-surges-in-february-2025-as-investor-sentiment-weakens-467694-2025-03-12)

### Inferences
- An emergency fund covering several months of expenses costs the spread between liquid-fund or FD yields and the expected equity return on that slice. This is a known, small cost. Its benefit is insurance against having to redeem equity at a bad time.
- The edge is high-probability in the sense of reducing the chance of a bad outcome. It does not raise expected returns in every state of the world.

### Gaps
- No Indian or global numeric study of forced-selling outcomes, e.g. loss crystallised by retail investors who redeemed in March 2020, was found in this session. AMFI data on net equity outflows in March–April 2020 could be used as a proxy.
